01
Core problem framing
Primary angle
Safe water is not the same as optimised water. Melbourne's supply meets standards — but treated water still contains chlorine, sediment, trace metals, and hard minerals that affect daily life.
Shower exposure angle
The shower is where most exposure happens. Skin and lungs absorb far more chlorine during a 10-minute shower than from drinking a glass of water. Most people focus on drinking water — this is the overlooked angle.
Coverage gap angle
Point-of-use filters treat one tap only. Every shower, bath, laundry, and dishwasher still runs on unfiltered water. "Most homes aren't fully protected."
Frequency angle
"Your family is in contact with tap water hundreds of times every day." Frequency builds stakes without fearmongering — the risk is cumulative, not acute.

Appointment incentive
"Your appointment qualifies for 20% off — please ensure all decision-makers are present." Discount tied to attendance. Creates household urgency and buy-in without discounting the brand broadly.
Pricing position
"We're not the cheapest option, and we're not priced at a premium — we sit in the middle." Targets the quality-conscious buyer who doesn't want to overpay. Paired with: "0% interest plans for eligible homeowners."
Visit structure
Arrive (5 min) → Test water live (10 min) → Walk through options (15 min). Transparency of the process is a selling point in itself.
Friction removers
No obligation to decide on the day. No follow-up hounding. Can call back weeks later. Fits in your afternoon. Confirm day before. Each one addresses a specific hesitation explicitly.

Ads short-form
Single angle only. Shower stat, gap hook, or "wish they'd done it sooner" social proof. One claim → one CTA. Match angle to audience awareness.
04
Tone of voice
Style
Short declarative sentences. Confident but never pushy. Information-first — what to expect, then why it matters. Reassurance woven in throughout, not added at the end. Addresses reader as "you" and "your home" consistently.
Language pattern
Pairs of fragments: "Clear. Simple." Clarifying em dashes. Numbers used for specificity (6×, 10 min, 7 years). Contrast structure: "It works — but..." / "Safe — but not the same as optimised."

Universal structural rule
Every section that introduces a problem or risk ends with a resolution — either a Neptune solution, a trust credential, or an explicit reassurance statement. The brand never leaves the reader in an anxious state without an exit.
Closing pattern
The page ends with a positive reframe of the decision already made ("You've made a smart decision") rather than a final pitch. The checklist — No obligation · Your questions answered · Licensed professionals · WaterMark certified — restates trust, not urgency.
