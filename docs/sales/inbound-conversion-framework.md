## SCRIPT ANALYSIS: WHAT'S WORKING & WHY

Before the framework, here's the strategic breakdown of what the reference scripts do psychologically — and where the ceiling is.

**What converts in both scripts:**
- Social proof anchoring ("we install a lot in your area") — normalises the purchase
- Problem seeding before the ask (chlorine → skin/hair) — creates emotional urgency
- Free assessment framing — removes financial risk perception
- Binary time-slot close — eliminates decision paralysis
- Partner requirement — increases close rate at in-home
- Promo positioned after the close — rewards commitment, doesn't bribe the decision

**Where both scripts leave conversion on the table:**
- No genuine rapport before the pitch — feels transactional within 30 seconds
- Problem anchor is blunt, not earned — "hair loss" lands awkwardly without setup
- No curiosity gap — customer is told everything, so there's less reason to book
- Price objection handling is thin — feels evasive rather than professional
- No tonality coaching — words without delivery is half a script
- No appointment protection strategy — cancellations bleed the close rate
- No SMS follow-up architecture — ghosting goes unaddressed
- No reactivation — dead leads stay dead

The Neptune framework below addresses every gap.

# PART 1: THE PHILOSOPHY

## The Fundamental Reframe

The goal of this call is **not** to sell a filtration system.

The goal is to make the homeowner feel like having someone come to their home is the **obvious, sensible, low-risk** next step — because they've just spoken to the most knowledgeable, trustworthy person they've ever encountered on the topic of their water.

The in-home consultation closes the sale. This call **earns the right** to that meeting.

When the rep finishes this call, the homeowner should feel:
- "These people actually know what they're talking about"
- "I had no idea about half of that"
- "It would be silly not to at least get the assessment done"
- "I already kind of like this company"

That feeling is worth more than any hard close technique.

## The Consultant Frame

The rep is not a salesperson. They are a **water quality consultant** helping a homeowner understand a situation that affects their family every single day.

This frame changes everything:
- Consultants ask questions before offering solutions
- Consultants don't pressure — they educate and advise
- Consultants are confident without being pushy
- Consultants make the customer feel heard

Operate entirely from within this frame. Never break it.

# PART 2: TONALITY COACHING

## This Is 70% Delivery, 30% Words

The script is the skeleton. Tonality is the muscle. Without the right delivery, even a perfect script sounds like a call centre.

## The Four Tonal Registers

**1. Warm Curiosity** — used during rapport and discovery
- Pace: slightly slower than normal conversation
- Energy: genuinely interested, not performative
- Example cue: asking about their water concerns — sound like you actually want to know

**2. Calm Authority** — used when explaining water quality, the assessment process, positioning
- Pace: measured, deliberate, no rushing
- Energy: confident, factual, unrattled
- Example cue: explaining why you can't give a price on the phone — sounds logical, not defensive

**3. Light Warmth** — used throughout, especially after the close
- Pace: natural
- Energy: relaxed, friendly, like someone wrapping up a good conversation
- Example cue: confirming the appointment — make them feel good about the decision

**4. Grounded Confidence** — used during objection handling
- Pace: no change — don't speed up or slow down when pushed
- Energy: unphased, empathetic, in control
- Example cue: "I completely get that" before pivoting — not defensive, not apologetic

## Pacing Rules

- **Slow down at key moments** — before planting a problem anchor, pause half a beat
- **Don't fill silence** — after asking a question, wait. Silence is productive.
- **Match their energy first, then lead them lower** — if they're anxious or fast-talking, briefly meet their pace, then consciously slow yours. They'll follow.
- **Smile when you speak** — it changes your vocal tone physically. Customers hear it.
- **Never rush the close** — a rushed close signals anxiety. Slow down at the appointment step.

## Language Rules

- Say "your water" not "the water" — ownership creates emotional connection
- Say "your home" not "the property" — warmth over corporate
- Say "one of our water technicians" not "a rep" or "a salesperson"
- Say "we'd love to get someone out to you" not "I need to book you in"
- Say "most people we speak to" not "everyone" — specificity is credible
- Avoid filler affirmations: "absolutely", "definitely", "100%" — they signal call-centre energy
- Preferred affirmations: "yeah", "gotcha", "makes sense", "exactly"

# PART 3: THE FULL INBOUND SCRIPT

> Script is modular. Each section is labelled. Read the psychology note before internalising each section.
>
> **Sequence logic:** Every section creates a question in the customer's mind that the next section answers. Follow the order — it is engineered to build curiosity and lower resistance progressively.

## SECTION 1 — OPENING

**[Psychology: The first 8 seconds determine whether you get a conversation or a wall. Sound like a person, not a process. The opener must feel like a human picked up the phone — not a call centre clicking into a dialler.]**

> "Hey, is this [Name]?"

*(Wait for confirmation)*

> "Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune. How are you going?"

*(Genuine pause — actually listen to their answer. A half-second of warmth here is worth more than any line in the script.)*

> "Good to hear. So I'm just following up on your enquiry about the water filtration — I just wanted to have a quick chat, get a bit of an understanding of your situation, and then work out the best way to help from there."

**Tonality note:** Unhurried. Natural. "Work out the best way to help" signals advisor, not seller. "Quick chat" lowers their guard — it says this isn't a pitch.

---

## SECTION 2 — DISCOVERY

**[Psychology: Before positioning, before problem anchoring — you need them talking. The more a customer speaks in the first two minutes, the more they trust you by the end of the call. Listen without loading your response. Silence after their answer is fine — it signals you actually absorbed what they said.]**

> "So first off — what was the main thing that got you looking into it? Any specific concerns, or just looking to improve things generally?"

*(Listen fully. Don't interrupt. Don't pre-load.)*

---

**[If they name a specific problem — skin, hair, taste, smell, discolouration:]**

> "Yeah, that makes a lot of sense — honestly, it's more common than people realise. Most of the time people have been dealing with it for a while without ever connecting it back to the water."

*(Brief pause. Let it land. Then move to Section 3.)*

**Why this works:** You've validated their concern without over-explaining, without mentioning location (we don't know it yet), and without sounding like you've heard it a hundred times. The phrase "without ever connecting it back to the water" plants a curiosity seed — they start wondering: *is that what's been happening to me?*

---

**[If they say "just generally" or "just curious" or "looking to improve":]**

> "Yeah, that's how most people start actually — just a feeling that the water could be better, or they've been reading more about it and want to know where they stand."

*(Then move to Section 3.)*

**Why this works:** You've normalised their vague motivation — they don't feel embarrassed for not having a concrete reason. They feel like a sensible person making a reasonable enquiry.

---

## SECTION 3 — LOCATION, OWNERSHIP & LOCAL AUTHORITY

**[Psychology: These qualifying questions must feel like one flowing beat, not a form. The suburb question does double duty — it qualifies the lead AND gives you the information you need to establish localised credibility. The local authority beat lands IMMEDIATELY after you have the suburb — this is the first moment it becomes credible, not before.]**

> "Whereabouts in Melbourne are you based?"

*(After answer — pause naturally, then:)*

> "Yeah, we're actually out [suburb] way quite a bit."

*(Delivered casually — stated as a fact, not a sales claim. One beat. No expansion yet.)*

> "And that's your own place, yeah?"

*(If yes — continue. If renting — see Objection Handling: Renter.)*

> "And roughly how many people are in the home with you?"

*(This is system-sizing data AND emotional leverage. A family of four is drinking, cooking, and showering in this water every single day. Let that reality sit quietly in the background.)*

**Why the sequence matters here:** The local authority line — *"we're out [suburb] way quite a bit"* — is earned because you now know the suburb. It doesn't feel like a scripted reassurance. It sounds like a natural observation from someone who actually does work in that area. It primes the next section: when the problem anchor references their suburb, it carries real weight.

---

## SECTION 4 — PROBLEM ANCHOR

**[Psychology: This is the emotional centre of the call. You are not creating alarm — you are gently reflecting a reality the customer likely hasn't considered. The local credibility established in Section 3 is what makes this land: you're not speaking about Melbourne generically, you're speaking about their suburb specifically. Deliver this at a slightly slower pace. Let the information breathe.]**

> "One thing that comes up pretty consistently out [suburb] way — and honestly across most of Melbourne — is that the chlorine concentration is actually quite a bit higher than most people realise."

*(Half-beat pause.)*

> "And it doesn't show up the same way for everyone. Some people notice it in the taste. Others feel it on their skin — dry patches, itchiness, sometimes it shows up in the hair and scalp. A lot of people have just gotten used to it over time without ever making the connection."

*(Now personalise:)*

> "Have you noticed anything like that yourself, or with anyone else in the home?"

---

**[If YES:]**

> "Yeah, that makes sense — once you understand what's actually going on with the water, it tends to click pretty quickly."

*(Don't over-expand. Let them sit with the connection they just made.)*

---

**[If NO:]**

> "Yeah, fair enough — it can be pretty subtle. Or it just becomes part of the background and people stop noticing it over time. Doesn't necessarily mean it's not there."

*(Don't expand further. Let that sit and move naturally into Section 5. The curiosity is planted — the assessment will address it in due course.)*

---

## SECTION 5 — WATER HABITS

**[Psychology: This is a discovery beat, not a pitch setup. The question does two things: it reveals awareness level and it keeps the customer talking. Elite closers use this section to listen and gather — not to explain or justify. The rep's responses should be short. The customer should be doing most of the talking here. Cost justification, savings comparisons, and education belong at the in-home — not here.]**

> "Are you mainly on tap water at home, or do you go through bottled?"

---

### If bottled water:

**[Signal: they're already aware something's off. They've taken action. Strong buying intent. Your job is to understand the WHY — not narrate their situation back to them.]**

The right move is curiosity. Pick the response that fits naturally in the moment:

**Option A — explore how long:**
> "Yeah — how long have you been doing that?"

*(Listen. Months = mild concern. Years = deep dissatisfaction. The longer, the stronger the signal.)*

**Option B — explore the reason:**
> "Yeah — was that more about the taste, or just a general thing?"

*(This surfaces the emotional driver. Taste, smell, skin concerns, or general distrust of tap — each tells you something about what will resonate at the in-home.)*

**Option C — if they've been doing it a long time and you want to go deeper:**
> "Right. What made you start — was there something specific?"

*(Used when Option A reveals a long timeframe. Gets the story behind the behaviour.)*

---

**After they answer — transition naturally:**
> "Yeah, makes sense."

*(One beat. Then move to Section 6. Don't add to it. The cost justification conversation belongs at the in-home — not here.)*

---

### If tap water:

**[Signal: lower awareness or higher tolerance. They may have a mild concern they haven't acted on, or no concern at all. The right move is gentle curiosity — not a lecture about what's in their water. Let them surface any underlying doubt themselves.]**

**Option A — invite self-reflection:**
> "Yeah, most people are. Has anything ever made you think twice about it?"

*(Non-pressuring. Gives them room to voice a concern they may not have articulated yet. If yes — follow it. If no — that's fine too.)*

**Option B — softer diagnostic:**
> "Yeah, gotcha. No real issues you've noticed, or just not something you've looked into?"

*(This phrasing makes "I've noticed nothing" feel like a perfectly reasonable answer. No pressure. But it also gives someone with a mild, unvoiced concern an easy opening.)*

**Option C — if they mention they've considered switching:**
> "What's been putting you off?"

*(Simple. Gets the blocker out in the open early so it can be addressed.)*

---

**After they answer — transition naturally:**
> "Yeah, gotcha."

*(One beat. Move to Section 6. This is not the moment to explain what's in their water — that comes later, through the technician, with actual results in front of them.)*

---

**[What to listen for across both paths:]**
- **Specific complaints** (taste, smell, skin, hair) → high motivation, anchor to these at in-home
- **Long-term workaround behaviour** (years of bottled water) → strong dissatisfaction, strong close potential
- **Vague general interest** → neutral; focus on curiosity, not urgency
- **"I haven't really thought about it"** → low awareness; keep tone light, lean into the free/no-obligation framing

The rep's job in this section is to **listen and catalogue**, not explain. Everything heard here feeds the technician's approach at the in-home.

*(This creates quiet urgency without alarm. The phrase "every day without knowing" is enough.)*

---

## SECTION 6 — PRIOR TESTING

**[Psychology: This is the knowledge gap question. It almost always reveals that the customer has never had a proper water assessment — and that admission is what makes the assessment feel not just useful but necessary. If they have been tested before, you have an opening to reframe what the assessment actually is. Either way, this section creates the logical bridge from "I have a concern" to "there's a clear and obvious way to address it."]**

> "And have you ever had your water actually tested before — like a proper on-site assessment of what's in it?"

---

**[If NO — the most common answer:]**

> "Yeah, that's pretty typical — most people haven't. So you've got a concern, you're managing it one way or another, but you don't actually have a clear picture of what's going on."

*(Pause. Let that sit. Then:)*

> "That's exactly what we'd look to fix."

---

**[If YES:]**

> "Oh nice — good that you've had that done."

*(Acknowledge it genuinely. Don't minimise it or probe for weaknesses in the competitor's test.)*

> "The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it. That's really where the right recommendation comes from."

*(Brief pause.)*

> "A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — and those are two pretty different conversations."

*(Then continue to Section 7.)*

**Why this works:** You've agreed the test has value — no dismissal, no competitive positioning. The reframe is honest: a test is a data point, not a recommendation. The visit is about understanding the home well enough to recommend the right system for it. A customer can immediately see the logic. It doesn't feel like a sales move — it feels like something someone who actually knows what they're doing would say.

---

## SECTION 7 — ASSESSMENT FRAMING

**[Psychology: By this point, the customer has: acknowledged a problem, revealed how they're (imperfectly) dealing with it, and admitted they've never properly tested their water. The assessment is now the obvious answer — your job is simply to describe it in a way that feels expert, low-risk, and worth their time. Do NOT over-explain the system or the product. That is the technician's job. Your job is to make the assessment feel like a no-brainer.]**

> "So what we'd do from here — we'd get one of our water technicians out to you. They run a full on-site water quality assessment — completely free, no obligation."

> "They'll test your water, show you exactly what's in it — chlorine levels, sediment, anything else that's there — and walk you through what that actually means for your home."

> "From there, if there's anything that needs addressing, they'll recommend what suits your place and give you a fixed quote on the spot. But there's no pressure to make any decision on the day."

*(Brief pause.)*

> "At the very least, you'll actually know what's in your water — which most people genuinely don't."

**Key line: "No pressure to make any decision on the day."** Counter-intuitive but critical. The easier this meeting sounds, the more bookings you lock. The in-home consultant creates the closing environment — your job on this call is simply to get the appointment in the calendar.

---

## SECTION 8 — TRANSITION TO BOOKING

**[Psychology: Don't ask "would you like to book?" — that's a yes/no that invites a "let me think about it." Instead, treat the appointment as the self-evident next step and move toward it without asking for permission. The transition should feel like a natural continuation of the conversation, not a gear shift.]**

> "Let me just take a quick look at what we've got available and get something sorted for you."

*(Brief pause — sounds like you're actually checking)*

> "Before I lock something in — is there anyone else in the home who'd normally be across a decision like this? Partner, spouse?"

---

**[If yes:]**

> "Worth having them there if you can — just means you don't have to relay everything afterwards. The technician goes through it all in detail and gives the quote on the spot, so it's a lot smoother when everyone's across it together."

---

**[If no:]**

> "No worries at all — just wanted to check."

---

## SECTION 9 — APPOINTMENT CLOSE

**[Psychology: Binary choice close. Never ask "when suits you?" — open-ended questions produce hesitation and lost bookings. Two specific options create a micro-decision that assumes the appointment is happening. Offer the options, then wait. Do not fill the silence — it is working in your favour.]**

> "So I've got [Specific Day + Time, e.g. Thursday afternoon] or [Alternate, e.g. Saturday morning] — which of those works better for you?"

*(Wait. Silence is productive. The first person to speak loses.)*

---

**[Once they select:]**

> "Perfect. And just grab your address for me?"

*(Get address, confirm suburb.)*

> "Brilliant. So that's [Day] at [Time] — the technician will be with you for about 30 to 40 minutes. They'll run through everything properly and you'll have a clear picture by the end of it."

---

## SECTION 10 — PROMO

**[Psychology: The promotion is a reward for deciding — not a lever to extract the decision. It must come AFTER the appointment is locked, never before. Introducing it pre-close cheapens the brand and signals desperation. Post-close, it lands as a genuine bonus that makes them feel good about the decision they already made.]**

> "One thing worth mentioning — we do have a promotion running through [month]. If you decide on the day that you'd like to go ahead, there's up to 20% we can take off. Entirely up to you — no pressure at all."

---

## SECTION 11 — CLOSE OUT

**[Psychology: The final 30 seconds create the emotional memory of the call. Warm, organised, confident. They should hang up feeling like they've spoken to someone who actually knew what they were talking about, and made a sensible decision. That feeling is your most powerful cancellation prevention tool.]**

> "I'll send you a confirmation through now — time, address, and a link to our site if you want to have a look beforehand."

> "If anything comes up or you need to shift the time, just reply to that message and we'll sort it out. Otherwise we'll see you [Day]."

> "Appreciate your time [Name] — speak soon."

# PART 4: OBJECTION MASTERY FRAMEWORK

## The Core Principle

Objections are not rejections. They are requests for more information or reassurance — usually rooted in one of four things:

1. **Uncertainty** — they don't know enough to commit
2. **Past experience** — a previous bad sales interaction is colouring this one
3. **Habit** — they reflexively say "no" or push back as default
4. **Genuine concern** — they have a real, specific issue that needs addressing

The framework for all four is the same:
**Acknowledge → Reframe → Redirect**

Never argue. Never defend. Never over-explain.

---

## OBJECTION: "Can you just give me a price?"

**[The most common. Handle this with complete confidence — it must never feel evasive.]**

> "Yeah, completely understand — honestly, I'd love to give you a number right now."

> "The issue is, if I gave you a figure without actually knowing anything about your home, I'd basically be guessing. And if I guess too low, you get a surprise on the day. If I guess too high, I've put you off before we've even had a chance to look at your situation properly."

> "What the technician does is come out, look at your plumbing, your home size, work out exactly what you need — and then give you one fixed number on the spot. No surprises, no back and forth."

> "That way the quote is actually accurate for you, not just a ballpark that may or may not mean anything."

*(If they push again:)*

> "I'll be honest with you — the range we typically see varies quite a bit depending on the home. It's like asking a builder to quote a renovation without seeing the house. The assessment is really the only way to give you something that's actually useful."

*(Then pivot directly back:)*

> "What I can do is get the technician out and at least give you something concrete. I've got [Day] or [Alt] — what works better?"

**Psychology note:** "I'd love to give you a number right now" removes defensiveness. "If I guess too low / too high" makes the explanation feel like it's for THEIR benefit, not to avoid the answer.

---

## OBJECTION: "Just send me some info"

> "Yeah, I can definitely do that — the only thing is, what we'd send through is pretty general. It won't tell you anything specific about your water or your home."

> "Most people who go down that path end up asking more questions after reading it, and then we end up booking the assessment anyway."

> "Honestly, the assessment tells you more in 30 minutes than any document could — and it's free. It's probably worth just getting someone out first."

*(Then:)*

> "I've got [Day] or [Alt] — which of those could work?"

---

## OBJECTION: "I've already had a water test"

> "Yeah, that's pretty common actually. A lot of companies do a quick test as part of a broader sales pitch."

> "What we do is a bit more comprehensive — and honestly, the testing is only part of it. The main thing is understanding what those results actually mean for your home, and what, if anything, you'd need to do about it."

> "Most people who've had a test done still find the assessment genuinely useful — it fills in the gaps."

*(Then:)*

> "Worth at least having a look. Is [Day] or [Alt] doable for you?"

---

## OBJECTION: "Not really interested / just enquired out of curiosity"

> "Yeah, totally fair — and there's absolutely no obligation at all."

> "Honestly, most people who end up getting a system started out the same way — just curious, not really planning on doing anything. Then they see the results and it changes things pretty quickly."

> "Even if nothing comes of it, you'll at least know exactly what's in your water — which most people actually don't. Is that something you'd be open to?"

*(If still hesitant:)*

> "Look, if now's not the right time, that's completely fine. Would it be okay if I reached out in a few weeks when things have settled?"

---

## OBJECTION: "Too expensive / not in the budget right now"

> "Yeah, I completely hear that — and that's exactly why we don't do pricing over the phone."

> "The assessment is free, and it's really just about getting a proper picture of what's going on with your water. From there, if it's not the right time financially, that's totally okay."

> "At the very least you'll know what you're dealing with — and when the time is right, you'll know exactly what you need."

*(Then:)*

> "I've got [Day] or [Alt] — even just to get the information, is either of those doable?"

---

## OBJECTION: "Why does my partner need to be there?"

> "Yeah, good question. It's not a hard requirement at all — more of a recommendation on our end."

> "What we find is, the technician goes through quite a bit of detail, and gives a fixed quote on the spot. If someone isn't there, people usually end up needing to relay everything — which is fine, but it sometimes means booking a second visit anyway."

> "If your partner can be there, it just makes the whole thing a lot smoother. But if not, we can absolutely work around it."

---

## OBJECTION: "I'm renting"

> "Ah, gotcha. Yeah, it does need to be owner-occupied for us to install — so that's fair enough."

> "Is there any chance you're looking at purchasing somewhere, or is it more of a long-term rental situation?"

*(If purchasing:)*

> "In that case, worth keeping us in mind once you're settled. I can pop you on our list and reach out when the time's closer if that'd be useful?"

*(If long-term rental:)*

> "No worries at all — appreciate you having a chat. If anything changes down the track, feel free to reach back out."

---

## OBJECTION: "I've seen your competitor / already getting quotes"

> "Yeah, makes sense — it's a big decision, worth doing properly."

> "Without knowing who you've spoken to, I can't really compare — but what I'd say is: get our technician out, see what they put together, and compare properly. At least then you're looking at apples to apples."

> "We're pretty confident in what we do and the quality of what we install — so we're happy to let the assessment speak for itself."

*(Then:)*

> "I've got [Day] or [Alt] — worth at least getting our take on it?"

---

## OBJECTION: "I need to think about it"

> "Yeah, of course — makes complete sense."

> "Can I ask, is there anything specific you're weighing up? Just want to make sure you've got everything you need."

*(Listen. Address the specific concern. Then:)*

> "Look, the assessment is genuinely free and low-pressure — it might actually make the decision a lot easier. What's the worst that happens? You know more than you do now."

*(If still needs time:)*

> "Fair enough. Can I check back in with you [Day/time]? I'll give you a ring then — and if you're ready to get something booked, we'll sort it out."

# PART 5: APPOINTMENT PROTECTION SYSTEM

## The Problem

Booking the appointment is only half the battle. The gap between booking and appointment day is where cancellations, ghosting, and "reschedules" happen.

The goal of this section is to make cancelling feel psychologically uncomfortable — not by pressure, but by **pre-investment language** that makes the homeowner feel committed and excited before the technician even arrives.

## Cancellation Prevention: Language at Booking

Use these phrases at the close to build pre-appointment investment:

> "The technician will actually look up your area beforehand so they come prepared — they'll have a sense of what the water's like in [suburb] before they even walk in."

> "They're pretty thorough — most people say the assessment is the most useful 30 minutes they've spent on their home."

> "You'll have a proper picture of your water by the end of it — regardless of what you decide."

Each of these creates **anticipation** and **perceived value** before the visit. The homeowner now has something to look forward to — cancelling means losing something they were looking forward to.

## 24-Hour Pre-Appointment Reminder

## If They Try to Reschedule

> "Of course, no problem at all. Let me see what we've got. Can I ask — is the original time completely off the table or just that particular day?"

*(If just the day:)*

> "Let me see what I can do. I want to keep you with the same technician — they'll already have looked into your area beforehand, so it's worth keeping that continuity."

*(This makes rescheduling feel like a minor course correction, not a door opening to ghosting.)*

# PART 7: REACTIVATION SCRIPTS

> For leads who enquired but never booked, or booked and cancelled without rescheduling.

---

## Reactivation Call — Opening

**[Psychology: Don't reference what happened before with frustration or pressure. Treat it as a fresh conversation with an existing relationship. Warm, curious, zero guilt-tripping.]**

> "Hey [Name], it's [Rep Name] from Neptune — how are you going?"

*(Wait for response)*

> "Just wanted to check in — you had reached out a little while back about the water filtration and I wanted to see if that was still something on your radar, or if things had changed."
