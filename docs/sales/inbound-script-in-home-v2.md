# The Full Inbound Script — In-Home Water Test Version
### Transcribed 10 Nov 2026 from Stefan's printed, hand-annotated copy (Adobe Scan). This is the working version to dissect and refine. Handwritten margin notes are marked ✍️.

**Flow:** OPEN → DISCOVER → LOCATE + LOCAL AUTHORITY → PROBLEM ANCHOR → WATER HABITS → PRIOR TESTING → ASSESSMENT FRAME → TRANSITION → CLOSE → PROMO → OUT

---

## SECTION 1 — OPENING
*The first 8 seconds determine whether you get a conversation or a wall. Sound like a person, not a process.*

> "Hey, is this [Name]?"

*(Wait for confirmation.)*

> "Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration. How are you going?"

*(Genuine pause — actually listen to their answer.)*

> "Good to hear. So I'm just following up on your enquiry — wanted to ask a couple of quick questions and arrange a quote for you."

---

## SECTION 2 — DISCOVERY
*Before positioning anything — you need them talking. The more a customer speaks in the first two minutes, the more they trust you by the end of the call.*

> "So first off — are you having any main concerns with the water at the moment, or just looking for cleaner water in general?"

*(Listen fully. Don't interrupt. Don't pre-load.)*

**If specific problem (skin / taste / smell):**
> "Yeah, that makes a lot of sense — honestly, it's more common than people realise. Most of the time people have been dealing with it for a while without ever connecting it back to the water."

*(Brief pause. Let it land. Move to Section 3.)*

**If general / "just curious":**
> "Yeah, that's how most people start actually — just a feeling that the water could be better, or they've been reading more about it and want to know where they stand."

*(Move to Section 3.)*

---

## SECTION 3 — LOCATION, OWNERSHIP & LOCAL AUTHORITY
*These qualifying questions must feel like one flowing beat, not a form. The local authority line lands immediately after you have the suburb — not before.*

> "Whereabouts in Melbourne are you based?"

*(After answer — pause naturally, then:)*

> "Yeah, we're actually out [suburb] way quite a bit."

*(One beat. Casual. No expansion yet.)*

> "And you're the homeowner as well?"

*(If yes — continue. If renting — see Objection Handling.)*

> "If you don't mind me asking — roughly how many people are living in the home with you?"

> "And are you mainly on tap water, bottled, or some sort of filtered water already?"

*(Water habits folded in here as one question in this version.)*

---

## SECTION 4 — PROBLEM ANCHOR
*This is the emotional centre of the call. Local credibility from Section 3 is what makes this land. Deliver at a slightly slower pace. Let the information breathe.*

> "One thing that comes up pretty consistently out [suburb] and surrounding [suburbs] is that the chlorine concentration is actually quite a bit higher than most people realise."

*(Half-beat pause.)*

> "And it doesn't show up the same way for everyone. Some people notice it in the taste. Others feel it on their skin — dry patches, itchiness, sometimes it shows up in the hair and scalp. A lot of people have just gotten used to it over time without ever making the connection."

> "Guess — anyone in the home experiencing something like this?"

**If YES:**
> "Yeah, that makes sense — once you understand what's actually going on with the water, it tends to click pretty quickly."

*(Don't over-expand. Let them sit with the connection.)*

**If NO:**
> "Yeah, fair enough — it can be pretty subtle. Or it just becomes part of the background — people get used to it over time and stop noticing. Doesn't necessarily mean it's not there."

*(Do NOT mention "assessment" yet. Move to the next section.)*

---

## SECTION 6 — PRIOR TESTING
*The knowledge gap question. It almost always reveals they've never had a proper assessment — and that admission is what makes the assessment feel necessary.*

> "Has anyone come by to conduct a water quality assessment before?"

**If NO — most common:**
> "Yeah, that's pretty typical — most people haven't. So you've got a concern, you're managing it one way or another, but you don't actually have a clear picture of what's going on."

*(Pause. Let that sit. Then:)*

> "That's exactly what we'd look to fix."

**If YES:**
> "Oh nice — good that you've had that done."

> "The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it."

> "A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — two pretty different conversations."

---

## SECTION 7 — ASSESSMENT FRAMING
*By this point the customer has acknowledged a problem, revealed how they're imperfectly handling it, and admitted they've never properly tested their water. The assessment is now the obvious answer. Do not over-explain the product — that is the technician's job.*

> "What we typically recommend is to get your water tested — we offer this completely free. One of our technicians comes past your place, tests it, shows you the results on the spot, and gives you a suggestion if there's anything to address."

> "From there, if there's anything that needs addressing, they'll recommend what suits your place and give you a fixed quote on the spot. But there's no pressure to make any decision on the day."

> "At the very least, you'll actually know what's in your water — which most people genuinely don't."

---

## SECTION 8 — TRANSITION TO BOOKING
*Don't ask "would you like to book?" — that invites a "no." Treat the appointment as the self-evident next step.*

> "Let me see when someone can come by."

*(Brief pause — sounds like you're actually checking.)*

> "While my calendar loads — just so you know, there's two small requirements: about 30 to 40 minutes of your time, which allows us to run the test and go through the product, and anyone else involved in the decision needs to be there too — so yourself and your partner, if that's relevant."

**If yes (partner exists):**
> "Worth having them there if you can — just means you don't have to relay everything afterwards. The technician goes through it all in detail and gives the quote on the spot, so it's a lot smoother when everyone's across it together."

**If no:**
> "No worries at all — just wanted to check."

---

## SECTION 9 — APPOINTMENT CLOSE
*Binary choice close. Never ask "when suits you?" — open-ended questions produce hesitation. Offer two options, then wait. The first person to speak after this question loses.*

> "Any particular time or day suits best?"

✍️ **Stefan's margin note:** "What time are you & your partner home? When do they get home?"

> "Perfect. And just grab your address for me?"

✍️ *(Highlighted on the printed copy.)*

> "Brilliant. So that's [Day] at [Time] — I'll send a confirmation through via text and email, with the time, address, a link to our site if you want to have a look beforehand, and a bit about who we are and what to expect when the technician arrives."

---

## SECTION 10 — PROMO
*Post-close only. The promotion is a reward for deciding — not a lever to extract the decision. Pre-close use cheapens the brand.*

> "Before I leave you to it — one thing worth mentioning — we do have a promotion running through [month]. If you decide on the day that you'd like to go ahead, there's up to 20% we can take off. Entirely up to you — no pressure at all."

---

## SECTION 11 — CLOSE OUT
*The final 30 seconds create the emotional memory of the call. Warm, organised, confident. This is your most powerful cancellation prevention tool.*

> "Anyways [Name], appreciate your time. Have a great rest of your day."
