Inbound Conversion Framework

Elite Inbound Phone Script — Psychological Architecture — Full Reference
Version 1.3  ·  May 2026  ·  Study between calls — not for use on desk during live calls

Script Analysis: What Works & Why

What converts

  • Social proof anchoring — normalises the purchase
  • Problem seeding before the ask — creates emotional urgency
  • Free assessment framing — removes financial risk perception
  • Binary time-slot close — eliminates decision paralysis
  • Partner requirement — increases close rate at in-home
  • Promo positioned after the close — rewards commitment, doesn't bribe

Where the ceiling was

  • No genuine rapport before the pitch — felt transactional inside 30 seconds
  • Problem anchor blunt, not earned — landed awkwardly without setup
  • No curiosity gap — told everything, so less reason to book
  • Price objection thin — felt evasive rather than professional
  • No tonality coaching — words without delivery is half a script
  • No appointment protection strategy or SMS architecture

Part 1 — The Philosophy

The Fundamental Reframe

The goal of this call is not to sell a filtration system. The goal is to make the homeowner feel like having someone come to their home is the obvious, sensible, low-risk next step — because they've just spoken to the most knowledgeable, trustworthy person they've ever encountered on the topic of their water.

The in-home consultation closes the sale. This call earns the right to that meeting.

When the call works, the homeowner hangs up thinking:

  • "These people actually know what they're talking about"
  • "I had no idea about half of that"
  • "It would be silly not to at least get the assessment done"
  • "I already kind of like this company"

That feeling is worth more than any hard close technique.

The Consultant Frame

The rep is not a salesperson. They are a water quality consultant helping a homeowner understand a situation that affects their family every single day.

  • Consultants ask questions before offering solutions
  • Consultants don't pressure — they educate and advise
  • Consultants are confident without being pushy
  • Consultants make the customer feel heard

Operate entirely from within this frame. Never break it.

Part 2 — Tonality Coaching

This Is 70% Delivery, 30% Words

The script is the skeleton. Tonality is the muscle. Without the right delivery, even a perfect script sounds like a call centre.

The Four Tonal Registers

Register When to use Character
Warm CuriosityRapport and discoverySlightly slower, genuinely interested — sounds like you actually want to know
Calm AuthorityWater quality, assessment process, positioningMeasured, deliberate, unrattled — confident and factual
Light WarmthThroughout, especially after the closeNatural, relaxed — like wrapping up a good conversation
Grounded ConfidenceObjection handlingNo pace change — unphased, empathetic, in control

Pacing Rules

  • Slow down at key moments — before the problem anchor, pause half a beat
  • Don't fill silence — after asking a question, wait
  • Match their energy, then lead lower — they'll follow
  • Smile when you speak — it changes vocal tone physically
  • Never rush the close — a rushed close signals anxiety

Language Rules

  • Say "your water" not "the water"
  • Say "your home" not "the property"
  • Say "one of our water technicians" not "a rep"
  • Say "most people we speak to" not "everyone"
  • Avoid: "absolutely", "definitely", "100%"
  • Preferred: "yeah", "gotcha", "makes sense"

Part 3 — The Full Inbound Script

Sequence logic: Every section creates a question in the customer's mind that the next section answers. Follow the order — it is engineered to build curiosity and lower resistance progressively.

OPEN → DISCOVER → LOCATE + LOCAL AUTHORITY → PROBLEM ANCHOR → WATER HABITS → PRIOR TESTING → ASSESSMENT FRAME → TRANSITION → CLOSE → PROMO → OUT
Section 1

Opening

The first 8 seconds determine whether you get a conversation or a wall. Sound like a person, not a process.
"Hey, is this [Name]?"

Wait for confirmation.

"Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune. How are you going?"

Genuine pause — actually listen to their answer.

"Good to hear. So I'm just following up on your enquiry about the water filtration — I just wanted to have a quick chat, get a bit of an understanding of your situation, and then work out the best way to help from there."
Section 2

Discovery

Before positioning anything — you need them talking. The more a customer speaks in the first two minutes, the more they trust you by the end of the call.
"So first off — what was the main thing that got you looking into it? Any specific concerns, or just looking to improve things generally?"

Listen fully. Don't interrupt. Don't pre-load.

If specific problem (skin / taste / smell)

"Yeah, that makes a lot of sense — honestly, it's more common than people realise. Most of the time people have been dealing with it for a while without ever connecting it back to the water."

Brief pause. Let it land. Move to Section 3.

If general / "just curious"

"Yeah, that's how most people start actually — just a feeling that the water could be better, or they've been reading more about it and want to know where they stand."

Move to Section 3.

Section 3

Location, Ownership & Local Authority

These qualifying questions must feel like one flowing beat, not a form. The local authority line lands immediately after you have the suburb — not before.
"Whereabouts in Melbourne are you based?"

After answer — pause naturally, then:

"Yeah, we're actually out [suburb] way quite a bit."

One beat. Casual. No expansion yet.

"And that's your own place, yeah?"

If yes — continue. If renting — see Objection Handling.

"And roughly how many people are in the home with you?"
Section 4

Problem Anchor

This is the emotional centre of the call. Local credibility from Section 3 is what makes this land. Deliver at a slightly slower pace. Let the information breathe.
"One thing that comes up pretty consistently out [suburb] way — and honestly across most of Melbourne — is that the chlorine concentration is actually quite a bit higher than most people realise."

Half-beat pause.

"And it doesn't show up the same way for everyone. Some people notice it in the taste. Others feel it on their skin — dry patches, itchiness, sometimes it shows up in the hair and scalp. A lot of people have just gotten used to it over time without ever making the connection."
"Have you noticed anything like that yourself, or with anyone else in the home?"

If YES

"Yeah, that makes sense — once you understand what's actually going on with the water, it tends to click pretty quickly."

Don't over-expand. Let them sit with the connection.

If NO

"Yeah, fair enough — it can be pretty subtle. Or it just becomes part of the background — people get used to it over time and stop noticing. Doesn't necessarily mean it's not there."

Do NOT mention "assessment" yet. Move to Section 5.

Section 5

Water Habits

Discovery beat, not a pitch setup. The rep's responses should be short. Ask ONE follow-up, then move. Cost justification belongs at the in-home — not here.
"Are you mainly on tap water at home, or do you go through bottled?"

If bottled — explore, then move

Option A: "Yeah — how long have you been doing that?"

Option B: "Was that more about taste or just a general thing?"

Option C (if long time): "What made you start — was there something specific?"

After answer → "Yeah, makes sense." → Move to Section 6.

If tap — light curiosity

Option A: "Has anything ever made you think twice about it?"

Option B: "No real issues noticed, or just not something you've looked into?"

Option C (considering switching): "What's been putting you off?"

After answer → "Yeah, gotcha." → Move to Section 6.

Section 6

Prior Testing

This is the knowledge gap question. It almost always reveals they've never had a proper assessment — and that admission is what makes the assessment feel necessary.
"And have you ever had your water actually tested before — like a proper on-site assessment of what's in it?"

If NO — most common

"Yeah, that's pretty typical — most people haven't. So you've got a concern, you're managing it one way or another, but you don't actually have a clear picture of what's going on."

Pause. Let that sit. Then:

"That's exactly what we'd look to fix."

If YES

"Oh nice — good that you've had that done."
"The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it."
"A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — two pretty different conversations."
Section 7

Assessment Framing

By this point the customer has acknowledged a problem, revealed how they're imperfectly handling it, and admitted they've never properly tested their water. The assessment is now the obvious answer. Do not over-explain the product — that is the technician's job.
"So what we'd do from here — we'd get one of our water technicians out to you. They run a full on-site water quality assessment — completely free, no obligation."
"They'll test your water, show you exactly what's in it — chlorine levels, sediment, anything else that's there — and walk you through what that actually means for your home."
"From there, if there's anything that needs addressing, they'll recommend what suits your place and give you a fixed quote on the spot. But there's no pressure to make any decision on the day."
"At the very least, you'll actually know what's in your water — which most people genuinely don't."
Section 8

Transition to Booking

Don't ask "would you like to book?" — that invites a "no." Treat the appointment as the self-evident next step.
"Let me just take a quick look at what we've got available and get something sorted for you."

Brief pause — sounds like you're actually checking.

"Before I lock something in — is there anyone else in the home who'd normally be across a decision like this? Partner, spouse?"

If YES

"Worth having them there if you can — just means you don't have to relay everything afterwards. The technician goes through it all in detail and gives the quote on the spot, so it's a lot smoother when everyone's across it together."

If NO

"No worries at all — just wanted to check."

Section 9

Appointment Close

Binary choice close. Never ask "when suits you?" — open-ended questions produce hesitation. Offer two options, then wait. The first person to speak after this question loses.
"So I've got [Specific Day + Time] or [Alternate] — which of those works better for you?"
"Perfect. And just grab your address for me?"
"Brilliant. So that's [Day] at [Time] — the technician will be with you for about 30 to 40 minutes. They'll run through everything properly and you'll have a clear picture by the end of it."
Section 10

Promo

Post-close only. The promotion is a reward for deciding — not a lever to extract the decision. Pre-close use cheapens the brand.
"One thing worth mentioning — we do have a promotion running through [month]. If you decide on the day that you'd like to go ahead, there's up to 20% we can take off. Entirely up to you — no pressure at all."
Section 11

Close Out

The final 30 seconds create the emotional memory of the call. Warm, organised, confident. This is your most powerful cancellation prevention tool.
"I'll send you a confirmation through now — time, address, and a link to our site if you want to have a look beforehand."
"If anything comes up or you need to shift the time, just reply to that message and we'll sort it out. Otherwise we'll see you [Day]."
"Appreciate your time [Name] — speak soon."

Part 4 — Objection Mastery

Core Principle

Objections are not rejections. They are requests for information or reassurance — rooted in uncertainty, past experience, habit, or genuine concern.

Acknowledge  →  Reframe  →  Redirect

Never argue. Never defend. Never over-explain.

OBJECTION: "Can you just give me a price?"
Strategy: Reframe price avoidance as professional integrity — for their benefit
"Yeah, completely understand — honestly, I'd love to give you a number right now."
"The issue is, if I gave you a figure without actually knowing anything about your home, I'd basically be guessing. And if I guess too low, you get a surprise on the day. If I guess too high, I've put you off before we've even had a chance to look at your situation properly."
"What the technician does is come out, look at your plumbing, your home size, work out exactly what you need — and then give you one fixed number on the spot. No surprises."
"That way the quote is actually accurate for you, not just a ballpark that may or may not mean anything."

If they push again:

"I'll be honest — the range varies quite a bit depending on the home. It's like asking a builder to quote a renovation without seeing the house."
"What I can do is get the technician out and at least give you something concrete. I've got [Day] or [Alt] — what works better?"
OBJECTION: "Just send me some info"
"Yeah, I can definitely do that — the only thing is, what we'd send through is pretty general. It won't tell you anything specific about your water or your home."
"Most people who go down that path end up asking more questions after reading it, and then we end up booking the assessment anyway."
"Honestly, the assessment tells you more in 30 minutes than any document could — and it's free. It's probably worth just getting someone out first."

Close: "I've got [Day] or [Alt] — which of those could work?"

OBJECTION: "I've already had a water test"
Strategy: Acknowledge positively — no competitor criticism — pivot value to seeing the home
"Oh nice — good that you've had that done."
"The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it. That's really where the right recommendation comes from."
"A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — and those are two pretty different conversations."

Close: "Worth at least having a look. Is [Day] or [Alt] doable for you?"

OBJECTION: "Not really interested / just curious"
"Yeah, totally fair — and there's absolutely no obligation at all."
"Honestly, most people who end up getting a system started out the same way — just curious, not really planning on doing anything. Then they see the results and it changes things pretty quickly."
"Even if nothing comes of it, you'll at least know exactly what's in your water — which most people actually don't. Is that something you'd be open to?"

If still hesitant: "Look, if now's not the right time, that's completely fine. Would it be okay if I reached out in a few weeks when things have settled?"

OBJECTION: "Too expensive / not in the budget"
"Yeah, I completely hear that — and that's exactly why we don't do pricing over the phone."
"The assessment is free, and it's really just about getting a proper picture of what's going on with your water. From there, if it's not the right time financially, that's totally okay."
"At the very least you'll know what you're dealing with — and when the time is right, you'll know exactly what you need."

Close: "I've got [Day] or [Alt] — even just to get the information, is either of those doable?"

OBJECTION: "Why does my partner need to be there?"
"Yeah, good question. It's not a hard requirement at all — more of a recommendation on our end."
"What we find is, the technician goes through quite a bit of detail, and gives a fixed quote on the spot. If someone isn't there, people usually end up needing to relay everything — which is fine, but it sometimes means booking a second visit anyway."
"If your partner can be there, it just makes the whole thing a lot smoother. But if not, we can absolutely work around it."
OBJECTION: "I'm renting"
"Ah, gotcha. Yeah, it does need to be owner-occupied for us to install — so that's fair enough."
"Is there any chance you're looking at purchasing somewhere, or is it more of a long-term rental situation?"
If purchasing: "Worth keeping us in mind once you're settled. I can pop you on our list and reach out when the time's closer if that'd be useful?"
If long-term rental: "No worries at all — appreciate you having a chat. If anything changes down the track, feel free to reach back out."
OBJECTION: "I've seen a competitor / already getting quotes"
"Yeah, makes sense — it's a big decision, worth doing properly."
"Without knowing who you've spoken to, I can't really compare — but what I'd say is: get our technician out, see what they put together, and compare properly. At least then you're looking at apples to apples."
"We're pretty confident in what we do and the quality of what we install — so we're happy to let the assessment speak for itself."

Close: "I've got [Day] or [Alt] — worth at least getting our take on it?"

OBJECTION: "I need to think about it"
"Yeah, of course — makes complete sense."
"Can I ask, is there anything specific you're weighing up? Just want to make sure you've got everything you need."

Listen. Address the specific concern. Then:

"Look, the assessment is genuinely free and low-pressure — it might actually make the decision a lot easier. What's the worst that happens? You know more than you do now."

If still needs time: "Fair enough. Can I check back in with you [Day/time]? I'll give you a ring then — and if you're ready to get something booked, we'll sort it out."

Part 5 — Appointment Protection

Booking the appointment is only half the battle. The goal is to make cancelling feel psychologically uncomfortable — not through pressure, but through pre-investment language that makes the homeowner feel committed and excited before the technician arrives.

Cancellation Prevention — Language at Booking

"The technician will actually look up your area beforehand so they come prepared — they'll have a sense of what the water's like in [suburb] before they even walk in."
"They're pretty thorough — most people say the assessment is the most useful 30 minutes they've spent on their home."
"You'll have a proper picture of your water by the end of it — regardless of what you decide."

If They Try to Reschedule

"Of course, no problem at all. Let me see what we've got. Can I ask — is the original time completely off the table or just that particular day?"
If just the day: "Let me see what I can do. I want to keep you with the same technician — they'll already have looked into your area beforehand, so it's worth keeping that continuity."

Part 7 — Reactivation Scripts

For leads who enquired but never booked, or booked and cancelled without rescheduling.

Reactivation Call

Don't reference what happened before with frustration or pressure. Treat it as a fresh conversation with an existing relationship.
"Hey [Name], it's [Rep Name] from Neptune — how are you going?"
"Just wanted to check in — you had reached out a little while back about the water filtration and I wanted to see if that was still something on your radar, or if things had changed."

If still interested

"Yeah, makes sense. We've had a fair bit happening in your area lately — might be worth getting someone out now while we've got some availability. I've got [Day] or [Alt] — what could work?"

If "not right now"

"No worries at all. Can I ask — is it more a timing thing, or has something changed with your situation?"

Address the response, then offer a soft future close.

Reactivation SMS — First Touch

Hey [Name] — it's [Rep Name] from Neptune. Just checking in — you'd reached out a few weeks back about water filtration for your home. Wanted to see if that was still something you were thinking about, or if timing had changed. Happy to have a quick chat if useful. No pressure at all — just didn't want to leave you hanging. — Neptune Water Filtration

Hey [Name], Neptune Water here again — just a final check-in. We do have a current promotion running through [Month] with up to 20% off. Just worth mentioning in case the timing is better now. Free assessment, no obligation. Happy to get something sorted quickly if you're keen. — Neptune

The Mindset Before Every Call

You're not trying to sell anything on this call. You're having a genuine conversation with someone whose water — the thing they drink, cook with, shower in, and give to their kids every single day — may not be as clean as they think. You have access to a free service that gives them real answers. Your job is simply to offer it in a way that makes it easy for them to say yes.
