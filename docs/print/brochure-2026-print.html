Melbourne water
contaminants.

Melbourne's water supply is treated and generally meets Australian standards — but treated water isn't filtered water. Treatment leaves chlorine or chloramine behind, ageing pipes add sediment, and your suburb decides how much limescale you're dealing with.

Chlorine & Chloramines
Present · All suburbs
Chlorine dissipates quickly and is the primary disinfectant across most of Melbourne. Some suburbs use chloramine instead, a more stable compound that doesn't break down the same way and stays active at the tap. Chloramine needs a proper carbon block with extended contact time to remove — not a jug or fridge filter. Both affect skin, hair, and taste.
Limescale Minerals — Calcium & Magnesium
Variable · Suburb-dependent
Melbourne's water is generally softer than many other Australian cities, but calcium and magnesium minerals can still form limescale on shower screens, taps, kettles, and appliances. The extent varies by suburb, and limescale build-up adds wear to hot water systems and dishwashers over time.
Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)
Trace levels · Pipe & industrial source
VOCs — including trihalomethanes (THMs) formed when chlorine reacts with organic matter — are present in treated reticulated supplies. Removal requires dedicated coconut shell activated carbon.
Heavy Metals — Lead & Mercury
Trace · Older infrastructure
Lead enters water through older service connections and internal plumbing — particularly in homes built before the 1980s. There is no safe level of lead in drinking water for children. Reduction is supported through Neptune's dedicated scale-reduction carbon stage, with broader heavy metal reduction from the coconut shell carbon stage.
Sediment, Rust & Particulates
Present · All properties
Pipe scale and fine particulates enter through your supply line and internal plumbing. First point of capture in a staged filtration system — essential before carbon stages.

Material specification
Why coconut shell carbon matters.
Coconut shell activated carbon yields a microporous structure with surface area exceeding 1,000 m²/g. Coal-based carbon — standard in low-cost filters — has significantly lower micropore volume and reduced adsorption capacity for chlorine, chloramine, VOCs, and heavy metals.
