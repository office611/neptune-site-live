# Neptune Filtration — Ad Creative Framework

**Status:** Documentation only. No production changes.
**Purpose:** Strategic and visual framework for all paid ad creative.
**Applies to:** Meta (Facebook/Instagram), Google Display, YouTube, any future paid channel.

---

## Current Ad Audit — Full Assessment

### What Was Running

Five static Facebook ad sets were created. All share a fundamental strategic misalignment
with the new Neptune premium positioning.

---

### Ad 1 & 2 — "$2,895 Installed" (Blue Product Ads)
**Formats:** 1080×1280, 1080×1500, Story, Square

**What it shows:**
- Bright medium-blue gradient background
- Blue 3-stage filter unit (lab/industrial aesthetic)
- "WHOLE HOUSE FILTRATION" in bold white caps
- "$2,895" in dominant yellow text
- Variants include "APRIL OFFER / Usually $3,995" with strikethrough

**What works:**
- Clear price point (if price-led strategy is the goal — but it is not)
- "Melbourne Homes" geo-specificity

**What doesn't work:**
- **Price is the entire message** — attracts price shoppers, not quality customers
- Bright blue is not the Neptune brand colour — creates inconsistency with website
- Blue filter unit looks industrial/commercial, not residential premium
- No Neptune logo anywhere
- No trust signals (no WaterMark, no Licensed)
- "APRIL OFFER" and strikethrough pricing signals discount brand
- All-caps condensed typography is tradie-marketing aesthetic
- The "Usually $3,995" comparison suggests the normal price is undesirable

**Recommendation:** **Retire.** The strategic approach (discount/price-led) is incompatible
with premium positioning. The visual approach compounds this.

---

### Ad 3 — "Feel the Difference in Your Water" (Shower Lifestyle)
**Format:** Vertical variants

**What it shows:**
- Woman in shower, eyes closed, enjoying the experience
- Grey/dark background with teal-accented headline
- Benefit checklist: Softer skin / Better tasting water / Reduced hair loss / No chlorine smell
- Dark navy bottom bar: "WHOLE HOME WATER FILTERS / $2,895 Installed" in yellow

**What works:**
- ✅ Benefit-led headline and body (best strategy in the set)
- ✅ Lifestyle visual tells the right story (clean water = better experience)
- ✅ Teal headline accent adds sophistication
- ✅ Dark navy bottom bar aligns with website aesthetic
- ✅ The outdoor grey unit appears at bottom — more premium than blue lab unit

**What doesn't work:**
- Price still closes the ad in yellow — undercuts the premium setup
- The woman in the shower is likely stock imagery — check licence
- "Reduced hair loss" is a specific health claim — ensure this is substantiated
- No Neptune logo

**Recommendation:** **Rebuild** using this structure as the foundation.
Keep: benefit checklist + lifestyle photography + dark navy strip
Remove: yellow price callout — replace with CTA and trust credentials

**Format:** Vertical

**What it shows:**
- "STOP DRINKING TOXIC WATER" in large bold caps
- Hand holding a glass of visibly murky, brown-tinted water from a tap

  Sediment (×2), Pesticides

**What works:**

**What doesn't work:**
- ❌ "TOXIC" — explicitly against brand voice guidelines
- ❌ "DANGER LEAD" badge — fear-based alarm tactic
- ❌ Murky brown water — scare imagery contradicts calm, trustworthy brand
- ❌ The contaminant icon grid looks like an infomercial from 2009
- ❌ "STOP DRINKING" imperative is aggressive and paternalistic
- ❌ Price still closed in yellow
- ❌ No logo, no trust signals
- ❌ Visually the lowest-quality creative in the set

**Recommendation:** **Retire immediately.** This ad actively harms the Neptune brand
and should not be associated with the company under any positioning.

### Ad 5 — "Melbourne Only — Whole Home Filtration" (Installation Photo)
**Formats:** Tall, Square, Story

**What it shows:**
- Real-looking installation photography: grey outdoor unit with copper pipes in a garden
- Lush green garden backdrop, suburban home visible
- "MELBOURNE ONLY" in white caps (geographic hook)
- "WHOLE HOME FILTRATION / $2,895 Installed" — white/yellow

**What works:**
- ✅ Best visual creative in the set
- ✅ The outdoor grey unit in a real garden context is the most premium image
- ✅ "Melbourne Only" geographic hook creates specificity and relevance
- ✅ The copper pipe detail reads as premium and distinctive
- ✅ Photography treatment is natural and warm
- ✅ Story about installation ("it goes in your garden") is instantly understood

**What doesn't work:**
- Price still leads — remove or de-emphasise significantly
- No Neptune logo
- No trust signals
- "INSTALLED" in yellow feels like a retail price tag

**Recommendation:** **Rebuild this as the primary creative template.**
The photography approach and geographic hook are the foundation of the new direction.

## New Creative Strategy

### The Strategic Shift

| Old Approach | New Approach |
|-------------|-------------|
| Price-led ("$2,895") | Value-led (what they get, not what it costs) |
| Fear/danger (toxic water) | Education/curiosity (optimise your water) |
| Generic product image | Premium installation in context |
| No brand identity on ads | Logo + trust credentials on every ad |
| Bright generic blue | Neptune Deep + brand colour system |
| Heavy condensed caps typography | Clean brand typeface — consistent with website |
| Discount framing | Premium positioning — no discounts in creative |
| No trust signals | WaterMark · VIC Licensed · 7-Year Warranty |

### Target Customer Response (What the Ad Should Make Them Think)

Wrong: "Oh, it's $2,895 — let me compare that to other options."
Right: "This looks like a serious, professional company. I've been wondering about my water quality. I should book that free test."

## Copy Hooks Bank — Premium Positioning

### Curiosity Hooks (for cold audiences)
- "Melbourne's water meets the standard. It doesn't meet yours."
- "Your water looks clean. Most people stop there."
- "6× more chlorine is absorbed in a 10-minute shower than a glass of water."
- "Every tap. Every shower. What's actually flowing through them?"
- "We've tested water in homes across Melbourne. The results are consistent."

### Benefit Hooks (for warm audiences)
- "Notice the difference from day one."
- "Clean water from every tap and shower — not just one."
- "Your skin and appliances both notice within a week."
- "Drink straight from any tap. Cook, shower, clean — all filtered."

### Trust/Authority Hooks (for credibility building)
- "WaterMark certified. VIC Licensed & Insured. Melbourne's water specialists."
- "7-year workmanship warranty. We stand behind every installation."
- "Free water test. We come to you. No obligation."
- "Licensed Melbourne plumbers. Professional installation. Done properly."

### Process Hooks (for objection removal)
- "Free water test. Approximately 30 minutes. You are never pressured to decide."
- "We show you what's in your water. You decide what to do with that information."
- "Book. We arrive. We test. We explain. No hard sell."

## What to Never Include in Neptune Ads

| Element | Reason |
|---------|--------|
| "$2,895" or any price as primary visual | Price-shopping attracts wrong customer |
| "TOXIC", "DANGER", "STOP DRINKING" | Fear-based, off-brand, alarming |
| Murky/brown water imagery | Opposite of the brand promise |
| Strikethrough "was/now" pricing | Discount brand signals |
| "APRIL OFFER" or seasonal discount framing | Undermines premium positioning |
| Bright electric blue backgrounds | Not the Neptune colour |
| Heavy condensed all-caps typography | Generic tradie aesthetic |
| Missing logo | Zero brand recognition |
| No trust credentials | Missed trust-building opportunity |
| Stock imagery of generic families | Generic, impersonal |

## Paid Media Funnel — Ad Sequencing

### Cold Audience (First Touch)
Goal: Awareness + curiosity
Best templates: C (Education) or A (Installation)
Don't ask for anything yet — just introduce the brand and the water quality issue.

### Warm Audience (Engaged, visited site, 7–14 days)
Goal: Consideration + booking intent
Best templates: B (Lifestyle benefits) or A (Installation with CTA)
Now you can ask for the free test booking.

### Hot Retargeting (Visited booking page, didn't convert, 1–7 days)
Goal: Remove final objection
Best templates: D (Process transparency)
Address "I don't want a hard sell" directly.

### Existing Customer
Goal: Filter reorder
Template: Simple clean product image + "Time to replace your Neptune filters"
No price in visual — direct to product page.

## Testing Framework

Always run at minimum two creative variations simultaneously.
Test one variable at a time — never change headline AND visual simultaneously.

**Recommended first tests:**
1. Template A (Installation) vs. Template C (Education) — cold audience
2. Hook A ("Melbourne's water meets standards") vs. Hook B ("Notice the difference") — same template
3. With trust credentials bar vs. without — measure impact on CTR and cost per lead

**KPIs by ad type:**
| Metric | Cold Audience Target | Warm Audience Target |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|
| CTR (Link) | >1.0% | >2.0% |
| CPM | <$15 | <$20 |
| Cost per lead | <$30 | <$20 |
| Lead quality (appt rate) | Track and optimise | Track and optimise |
