Neptune Filtration

First call script v2 — the fast qualifying call, not the close. No price given here. Job of this call: build rapport, find out what they actually know, get photos, book a same-day Zoom with the right people on it.

1
Opening

Greeting First

Hey, is this [Name]? It's Stefan from Neptune Filtration — how you going?

Keep it plain and quick — this call is short by design, don't slow the opening down.
2
Story Arc

Let Them Self-Select

So look, most people who reach out to us fall into one of two camps — they've already sat down with one of these filtration companies and saw through the sales pitch, or they've heard enough about how it usually goes that they just want to skip straight past it. Where are you at?

Categorization, not interrogation — builds rapport fast, still works even though this call no longer moves toward giving a price.
3
If They've Sat With A Company

Follow-Up Questions

Which company was that, out of curiosity? What was it that put you off going ahead with them? And how long ago was that?

4
Dig Pain Point

What's Actually Going On

So tell me — what's actually been going on with your water? How long's it been bothering you? Have you tried anything already to help with it?

Keep this brief — one or two exchanges, not a full interview. The Zoom is where this gets used properly.
5
The Real Diagnostic

Find Out What They Actually Know

What have you already found out about us, and what's still unclear?

Never ask "did you read the page?" — self-report is worthless, everyone says yes. This question reveals it through the quality of their answer instead. Specific and coherent = informed. Vague, or asking something clearly on the page (price, how often filters get changed, etc.) = not informed.
If their answer shows they haven't read it
No worries — I'll cover all that for you now.
Do NOT actually answer their specific question here (e.g. price, filter changes) — that's the re-teaching that drags calls out. This line is just a smooth acknowledgment, then move straight into step 6 below. Everything they asked about gets covered properly on the Zoom.
6
Photos, The Zoom, Then Who Else Is In

One Continuous Beat, Not Three Separate Asks

Say It Plainly, Confidently

To get you an accurate price — not just a rough guess — I need a couple of photos of your meter and where the system would go, way easier than having someone come out to your house like other companies make you do. Once I've got those, we'll jump on a quick Zoom to confirm your exact price. Is this a decision you're making with anyone else — partner, wife? Let's get them on that Zoom too so we're not doing this twice.

Order matters: photos reason first (so it's clearly operational, not a stall), Zoom mentioned as the natural next step, THEN the decision-maker question — which now refers to something already on the table instead of landing as a separate, out-of-nowhere ask. Deliver as a stated fact, not a request up for negotiation.
7
Book It — Same Day, With A Real Gap

Offer Specific Times, Never "Whenever Works"

Once you've grabbed those photos — I've got time this afternoon, or this evening, which works better for you both?

Two rules at once: never open-ended ("when works for you" is how bookings drift to 5 days out) — but never immediate either, since they need real time to actually walk out and take the photos first. Build in at least a couple of hours minimum before either offered slot, not "in 20 minutes." Two specific same-day options, both realistically spaced after now.
If they push for something days out anyway
No worries, we can do that — I'll send a reminder closer to the time.
Book it if that's genuinely all that works, but treat it as lower-confidence — a person who wants something further out despite easier options existing is telling you something real about their urgency. Layer in nurture-style follow-up alongside it, don't treat it as a locked appointment.
8
Close This Call

Confirm And Get Off

Perfect — send those photos through whenever suits, and I'll see you both on the Zoom at [time]. We'll sort your exact price and go from there.

Keep it short. This call's only job is done: rapport built, photos requested, right people booked, same day. Everything else — price, objections, the actual ask — happens on the Zoom.
Hard Rule

Never ask a question which sounds unsure. Every line delivered with full confidence — hesitant delivery undermines the authority this whole call is built on.

What's intentionally not in this call

No price. No objection handling (price/product/me branches). No guarantee pitch. All of that belongs on the Zoom, where there's actually time to do it properly — cramming it into this short call is exactly what was causing calls to drag. See close-script.html for that stage.
