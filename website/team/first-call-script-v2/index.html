Neptune Filtration

First call script v2 — the fast qualifying call, not the close. No price given here. Job of this call: build rapport, get photos, book a same-day Zoom with the right people on it. Everything else — what they know, their questions, price — gets handled by nurture and the Zoom, not this call.

1
Opening

Greeting First

Hey, is this [Name]? It's Stefan from Neptune Filtration — how you going? You would've seen my face before you enquired.

Genuine callback — his face is actually shown in the ad and VSL, so this is true, not a claim. Keep it plain and quick regardless — this call is short by design, don't slow the opening down.
2
Story Arc

Let Them Self-Select

Quick one — have you already sat down with one of these filtration companies before, or is this your first time looking into it?

Direct, not a set-up — the old version wrapped this in a two-camp narrative before asking, which just delayed the actual question. Same branching purpose (sends them into step 3 or straight to step 4), asked in one breath.
3
If They've Sat With A Company

Follow-Up Questions

Which company was that, out of curiosity? What was it that put you off going ahead with them? And how long ago was that?

4
Dig Pain Point

What's Actually Going On

So tell me — what's actually been going on with your water? How long's it been bothering you? Have you tried anything already to help with it?

Keep this brief — one or two exchanges, not a full interview. The Zoom is where this gets used properly.
5
Confidence, Then The Zoom, Then Photos — One Thought

Why We Jump On A Zoom

Based on what you've told me, we can definitely help you get that sorted. Here's how it works — jump on a quick Zoom with me, I'll run you through exactly how we get you there, and give you your exact price. Only thing I need before then is a couple of photos of your place — your meter, and wherever you'd want it positioned if you've got a spot in mind — so instead of guessing, we come to that Zoom with your price and set-up already worked out for your home.

Rewritten as one causal thought instead of two separate announcements: confidence tied to their actual pain point first, then photos explained as the REASON for the Zoom (accurate price instead of a guess), not a disconnected extra ask tacked on after. Send those through now if you can.
"Just give me a price now, I don't want to waste your time"
You're not wasting my time at all — this is exactly how I make sure you get an accurate number instead of a guess I'd have to walk back later. A couple of minutes now saves us both a wasted conversation. Let's just grab those photos and get you locked in for [time].
Reframes their own consideration as the reason FOR the process, not a reason to skip it. Never give a rough/guessed number here — a guess that doesn't match the real price later undermines the whole "accurate, not a guess" positioning.
"What's standard at the meter, price mate? I already know"
Not something I'll throw out over the phone, mate — give you a number now and you'll be back with ten more questions before we've even started. Let's get you sat down properly on the Zoom, photos in hand, and I'll walk you through everything in one go.
Deliberately not confirmed here, even as a "standard" number — giving any figure on this call reopens exactly the cascade of follow-up questions the whole two-call structure exists to avoid. Redirect to the Zoom instead of answering, same as the general price pushback above it.
6
Decision-Maker Check

Its Own Moment, Not Squeezed In

Is this a decision you're making with anyone else — partner, wife? Let's get them on the Zoom too so we're not doing this twice.

Gets its own breath now instead of being buried at the end of a longer beat — feels considered, not rushed past.
7
Book It — Same Day, With A Real Gap

Offer Specific Times, Never "Whenever Works"

Once you've grabbed those photos, let's lock in this afternoon — or if that doesn't suit, this evening works too.

Lead with ONE slot as the default, stated like it's already decided — not "which works better for you both?" which hands over control and invites drift. The second time is only offered as a fallback if the first genuinely doesn't work, not presented as an equal open choice. Still same-day, still a couple of hours minimum after now to allow time for the photos.
If they push for something days out anyway
No worries, we can do that — I'll send a reminder closer to the time.
Book it if that's genuinely all that works, but treat it as lower-confidence — a person who wants something further out despite easier options existing is telling you something real about their urgency. Layer in nurture-style follow-up alongside it, don't treat it as a locked appointment.
8
Close This Call

Confirm And Get Off

Perfect — send those photos through now, and I'll see you both on the Zoom at [time]. We'll sort your exact price and go from there.

Keep it short. This call's only job is done: rapport built, photos requested, right people booked, same day. Everything else — price, objections, the actual ask — happens on the Zoom.
Hard Rule

Never ask a question which sounds unsure. Every line delivered with full confidence — hesitant delivery undermines the authority this whole call is built on.

What's intentionally not in this call

No price. No objection handling (price/product/me branches). No guarantee pitch. No "what have you found out / what's unclear" diagnostic — that only made sense back when the page itself was the only source of information; now the nurture sequence sends neptunefiltration.com.au/answers and pushes it repeatedly after this call, and the Zoom opener ("What final questions have you got?") does the live check instead. Asking it here, before any of that has happened, was asking about knowledge they couldn't have yet. All of the above belongs on the Zoom, where there's actually time to do it properly — cramming it into this short call is exactly what was causing calls to drag. See close-script.html for that stage.

Pricing — for reference, applies on the Zoom, not this call

Stated price is always $3,195. Never use the word "discount," on this call or the Zoom. If needed to close a hesitant buyer, quietly move to $2,995 on the Zoom — not announced or offered upfront, just the number if it's actually needed to get a yes.
