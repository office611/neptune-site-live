The inbound script

This is the call, start to finish. Blue blocks are what you say; italics are how to play it.

⬇ Download the script (PDF)

They asked us to call. You're the authority.

Every person you call enquired with us. They filled in the form for a reason. You're not interrupting them or wasting their time; you're getting back to them, like they asked.

  • Never apologise for calling. No "sorry to bother you", no "is now a bad time?"
  • Lead the call. You know the process; they don't. Ask the questions, guide them through, and book it in.
  • Speak with confidence. You're the one who can help them, so sound like it.

You'll do better than me, because you don't know filtration

I know too much about water and filters, so on the phone I have to act like I don't. You don't have to act. That's your advantage.

  • Your job isn't to sell filtration. It's to listen, follow the script and book the appointment. That's it.
  • Be the person they talk to. Ask about them, let them tell you their story, and care about it. Think good listener, not salesperson.

Before you start: don't be a robot. Be in flow.

The script is a map, not lines to recite. Customers can hear someone reading in the first five seconds, and they switch off. Learn it well enough that you can leave it and find your way back.

  • Listen more than you talk. React to what they actually said, and use their words back to them.
  • Match them. Chatty customer: have the chat. Direct customer: keep it short and get to the booking.
  • When they go off-script, go with them. Answer the question, then steer back. The Objections section shows how.
  • Sound like you'd sound talking to a mate. Relaxed, warm and confident. Smile. They can hear it.

1 — Opening

The first 8 seconds determine whether you get a conversation or a wall. Sound like a person, not a process.

"Hey, is this [Name]?"

Wait for confirmation.

"Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration. How are you going?"

Genuine pause — actually listen to their answer.

"Good to hear. So I'm just following up on your enquiry — wanted to ask a few quick questions and arrange a quote for you right away."

2 — Discovery

Before positioning anything — you need them talking. The more a customer speaks in the first two minutes, the more they trust you by the end of the call.

"So first off — are you having any main concerns with the water at the moment, or just looking for cleaner water in general?"

Listen fully. Don't interrupt. Don't pre-load.

If specific problem (skin / taste / smell)

"Yeah, that makes a lot of sense — honestly, it's more common than people realise. Most of the time people have been dealing with it for a while without ever connecting it back to the water."

Brief pause. Let it land.

If general / "just curious"

"Yeah, that's how most people start actually — just a feeling that the water could be better, or they've been reading more about it and want to know where they stand."

3 — Location, Ownership & Local Authority

These qualifying questions must feel like one flowing beat, not a form. The local authority line lands immediately after you have the suburb — not before.

"Whereabouts in Melbourne are you located?"

After answer — pause naturally, then:

"Yeah, we're actually out [suburb] way quite a bit."

One beat. Casual. No expansion yet.

"And you're the homeowner as well?"

If yes — continue. If renting — polite close out.

"If you don't mind me asking — how many people are living in the home with you?"
"And are you currently using the tap water for drinking, or do you buy bottled water, or is there a filter you use?"

4 — Problem Anchor

This is the emotional centre of the call. Local credibility from Section 3 is what makes this land. Deliver at a slightly slower pace. Let the information breathe.

"We do install quite a lot of systems out in [suburb] and surrounding [suburb], and what we typically find is that the chlorine concentration is quite high. So a lot of customers do experience side effects from the water — issues with their skin, and issues with their hair as well."
"So guess on that — is anyone in the home experiencing anything like that?"

If yes

"Yeah, that makes sense — once you understand what's actually going on with the water, it tends to click pretty quickly."

Don't over-expand. Let them sit with the connection.

If no

"Yeah, fair enough — it can be pretty subtle. Or it just becomes part of the background — people get used to it over time and stop noticing. Doesn't necessarily mean it's not there."

Do NOT mention the "assessment" yet. Move to the next section.

5 — Prior Testing

The knowledge gap question. It almost always reveals they've never had a proper assessment — and that admission is what makes the assessment feel necessary.

"Has anyone ever come by to do a water quality assessment before?"

If no — most common

"Yeah, that's pretty typical — most people haven't. So you've got a concern, you're managing it one way or another, but you don't actually have a clear picture of what's going on."

Pause. Let that sit. Then:

"That's exactly what we'd look to fix."

If yes

"Oh nice — good that you've had that done."
"The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it."
"A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — two pretty different conversations."

6 — Assessment Framing

By this point the customer has acknowledged a problem, revealed how they're imperfectly handling it, and admitted they've never properly tested their water. The assessment is now the obvious answer. Do not over-explain the product — that is the technician's job.

"What we typically recommend, [Name], in order for us to know which of our systems would suit you, is having a test done on your water first. The good thing is we offer the testing service completely free, so there's no cost involved for you."
"What we do is come past your place, conduct a quick test on the water, and show you those test results on the spot. Based on our findings, we'll then give you a suggestion on which of our systems, if any, would suit you best — ultimately it's up to yourself."
"If you do like the sound of that, we'll show you how it looks, the different options for installation, and as well provide you with a free quote. We also offer 0% interest payment plans on all of our systems, up to 36 months."

7 — Transition To Booking

Don't ask "would you like to book?" — that invites a "no." Treat the appointment as the self-evident next step.

"Let me see when someone can come by."

Brief pause — sounds like you're actually checking.

"While my calendar loads — just two things that we ask from you, [Name]. Firstly, just to allow us roughly 30 to 40 minutes of your time, which allows us to conduct the test on the water, run you through the products and give you that quote. And just secondly, that both decision-makers are available — so yourself and your partner."

If a partner exists

"Worth having them there if you can — just means you don't have to relay everything afterwards. The technician goes through it all in detail and gives the quote on the spot, so it's a lot smoother when everyone's across it together."

If not

"No worries at all — just wanted to check."

If they ask why, or say it's not needed

"The reason we ask is the technician goes through quite a lot of information — so it's a lot easier to have everyone involved there together."

If they say they're the decision-maker

"No dramas — just to clarify, you don't need your partner there for this one?"

If they confirm:

"No dramas — I'll let the technician know."

Put it in the notes: "Sole decision-maker."

8 — Appointment Close

Let them pick the day, then offer two specific times. Then wait — the first person to speak after this question loses.

"Is there any particular day or time that suits you guys best?"

Leave space here — let them talk, and work out a time together.

Once they give you a day, always give them two options:

"Is morning or afternoon better for you guys?"
"Perfect — I've got [time] or [time]. Which works better?"

If needed

"Do you work from home any days, or have any days off?"

When they agree on a time, repeat it back:

"Perfect — so that's yourself and your partner both available on [Day] at [Time]?"
"And just grab your address for me?"

Always repeat the address back — we cannot get this wrong.

"Just to confirm — that's [address]?"
"Brilliant, I'll book that one in for you now for [Day] at [Time]. I'll send you a confirmation message — in the text there'll be a link to our previous customers page. If you get a chance between now and [Day], have a look through it: you'll see feedback from people in your area, and photos so you can see exactly how and where your system will be installed, and you can have a discussion with the technician when they come past. If you or your partner do become unavailable, shoot me a text so we can reschedule you and give that timeslot to someone else."

9 — Promo

Post-close only. The promotion is a reward for deciding — not a lever to extract the decision. Pre-close use cheapens the brand.

"Perfect, thanks [Name]. Just one last thing before I let you go — we do have a promotion running for the month of [month], so we're offering up to 20% off all our systems. Of course, the test and the quote are free of charge and there's no obligation to proceed, but if you do like the sound of our products and reviews and want to go ahead straight away, you'd get up to 20% off the quoted price."

10 — Close Out

The final 30 seconds create the emotional memory of the call. Warm, organised, confident. This is your most powerful cancellation prevention tool.

"Anyways [Name], appreciate your time. Have a great rest of your day."

The Calendar

Booking by area

The technician drives to every appointment. How you book decides whether he does three jobs close together or spends five hours in the car. Videos 3 to 7 show it on screen.

Have these open on every call

  • GoHighLevel with the lead open, ready to call.
  • The calendar in a second tab, so you can see where the technician already is that day.
  • Google Maps, to look up their address and check travel time while they talk.
  • This page, plus the script printed in front of you and a notebook for notes.

Appointment times

Monday to Friday: 10am, 12pm, 2pm, 4pm, 6pm.
Saturday: 9am, 11am, 1pm, 3pm.

Stick to these slots. Only use a custom time if you really have to, and keep it close: 1:30 instead of 2:00, not a whole new time.

Which slots to offer first

  • Every appointment takes 2 hours once you add the test, the quote, signing up and finance, so travel time between jobs matters.
  • Ask "morning or afternoon?", then give two times. For example, if they say afternoon: "I've got 2 o'clock or 4 o'clock."
  • Fill the midday slots first. They book out the slowest. The 4pm and 6pm slots fill fast on their own.
  • 6pm is for when it's a must, like when both of them work until late.
  • Only offer Saturdays if they can't do a weekday. Everyone wants Saturday mornings.

Reminders & cancellations

Once you book, customers automatically get a confirmation, a 24-hour reminder and a 1-hour reminder. If you book within 24 hours, they only get the confirmation and the 1-hour reminder.

If someone cancels, jump on it straight away. Call them and ask why. If it's the day before, work around it with a new time. If it's on the day: "The technician's already out your way."

Every lead costs us real money, so we book everyone we get in contact with and we save every cancellation we can.

One area per day

We split Melbourne into three areas. Try to keep each day in one area:

  • West: Tarneit, Truganina, Werribee, Williams Landing, Point Cook, Burnside, Caroline Springs, Harkness and surrounds.
  • North: Craigieburn, Doreen, Mernda, Mickleham, Kalkallo, Mill Park, Preston and surrounds.
  • South-East: Clyde, Cranbourne, Berwick, Officer, Pakenham, Narre Warren, Rowville, Dandenong and surrounds.

Inner suburbs (Caulfield, Avondale Heights, Northcote and so on) fit in on the way to or from an area.

How to pick the slot

  • Check the suburb before you call. It's already on the lead, under All Fields.
  • Look at the calendar. If the technician is already near them that day, offer that day first.
  • Check Google Maps. Look at the drive between appointments. Avoid booking the West and the North on the same day.
  • Metro Melbourne only. Far-out areas like Gippsland (Wonthaggi, for example) are too far, so we don't book them.
  • Not sure? Book it in anyway. If it doesn't work, Stefan will call you and you'll move it together. A booking is better than no booking.

Appointment notes: what to write

After booking, open the appointment and add the notes in the description. Keep them clean and simple:

  • What they're after: cleaner water in general, or a specific concern.
  • How many people live in the home.
  • Their concerns: skin, hair, taste, smell.
  • Anything small that helps the technician, like "sole decision-maker" or "already had a test from another company".

Never write negatives like "doesn't want to be pressured" or "won't decide on the day". The technician will walk in expecting a no. You don't need to write the suburb or homeowner status, because we already have those.

Where the lead goes after the call

  • Booked: moves to Booked In Home automatically once the appointment is saved.
  • Asked to be called later: drag to Re Contact and note when, like "call back in 2 weeks".
  • Spoke but couldn't book: drag to Contacted – Could Not Book In with a note on why. Call again in about a week.
  • No answer: drag to Contacted – Did Not Answer and run the follow-up.
