The inbound script

This is the call, start to finish. Blue blocks are what you say; italics are how to play it.

1 — Opening

The first 8 seconds determine whether you get a conversation or a wall. Sound like a person, not a process.

"Hey, is this [Name]?"

Wait for confirmation.

"Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration. How are you going?"

Genuine pause — actually listen to their answer.

"Good to hear. So I'm just following up on your enquiry — wanted to ask a couple of quick questions and arrange a quote for you."

If they ask the price straight away

"Great question — I'll come back to that, just two quick questions first so I point you at the right setup."

Then you must come back to it. See "If they ask the price" below.

2 — Discovery

Before positioning anything — you need them talking. The more a customer speaks in the first two minutes, the more they trust you by the end of the call.

"So first off — are you having any main concerns with the water at the moment, or just looking for cleaner water in general?"

Listen fully. Don't interrupt. Don't pre-load.

If specific problem (skin / taste / smell)

"Yeah, that makes a lot of sense — honestly, it's more common than people realise. Most of the time people have been dealing with it for a while without ever connecting it back to the water."

Brief pause. Let it land.

If general / "just curious"

"Yeah, that's how most people start actually — just a feeling that the water could be better, or they've been reading more about it and want to know where they stand."

If they mention eczema, a child's skin, or any illness: never say filtered water "will help" or "fix" it. Say: "Sorry to hear that — we hear about skin a lot. The technician can show you exactly what's in your water." Put it in the notes.

3 — Location, Ownership & Local Authority

These qualifying questions must feel like one flowing beat, not a form. The local authority line lands immediately after you have the suburb — not before.

"Whereabouts in Melbourne are you based?"

After answer — pause naturally, then:

"Yeah, we're actually out [suburb] way quite a bit."

One beat. Casual. No expansion yet.

"And you're the homeowner as well?"

If yes — continue. If renting — polite close out.

"If you don't mind me asking — roughly how many people are living in the home with you?"
"And are you mainly on tap water, bottled, or some sort of filtered water already?"

4 — Problem Anchor

This is the emotional centre of the call. Local credibility from Section 3 is what makes this land. Deliver at a slightly slower pace. Let the information breathe.

"One thing that comes up pretty consistently out [suburb] and surrounding [suburbs] is that the chlorine concentration is actually quite a bit higher than most people realise."

Half-beat pause.

"And it doesn't show up the same way for everyone. Some people notice it in the taste. Others feel it on their skin — dry patches, itchiness, sometimes it shows up in the hair and scalp. A lot of people have just gotten used to it over time without ever making the connection."
"Guess — anyone in the home experiencing something like this?"

If yes

"Yeah, that makes sense — once you understand what's actually going on with the water, it tends to click pretty quickly."

Don't over-expand. Let them sit with the connection.

If no

"Yeah, fair enough — it can be pretty subtle. Or it just becomes part of the background — people get used to it over time and stop noticing. Doesn't necessarily mean it's not there."

Do NOT mention "assessment" yet. Move to the next section.

5 — Prior Testing

The knowledge gap question. It almost always reveals they've never had a proper assessment — and that admission is what makes the assessment feel necessary.

"Has anyone come by to conduct a water quality assessment before?"

If no — most common

"Yeah, that's pretty typical — most people haven't. So you've got a concern, you're managing it one way or another, but you don't actually have a clear picture of what's going on."

Pause. Let that sit. Then:

"That's exactly what we'd look to fix."

If yes

"Oh nice — good that you've had that done."
"The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it."
"A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — two pretty different conversations."

6 — Assessment Framing

By this point the customer has acknowledged a problem, revealed how they're imperfectly handling it, and admitted they've never properly tested their water. The assessment is now the obvious answer. Do not over-explain the product — that is the technician's job.

"What we typically recommend is to get your water tested — we offer this completely free. One of our technicians comes past your place, tests it, shows you the results on the spot, and gives you a suggestion if there's anything to address."
"From there, if there's anything that needs addressing, they'll recommend what suits your place and give you a fixed quote on the spot. But there's no pressure to make any decision on the day."
"At the very least, you'll actually know what's in your water — which most people genuinely don't."

7 — Transition To Booking

Don't ask "would you like to book?" — that invites a "no." Treat the appointment as the self-evident next step.

"Let me see when someone can come by."

Brief pause — sounds like you're actually checking.

"While my calendar loads — just so you know, there's two small requirements: about 30 minutes of your time, which allows us to run the test and go through the product, and anyone else involved in the decision needs to be there too — so yourself and your partner, if that's relevant."

If a partner exists

"Worth having them there if you can — just means you don't have to relay everything afterwards. The technician goes through it all in detail and gives the quote on the spot, so it's a lot smoother when everyone's across it together."

If the partner works late

"No stress — we go till about 6:30 on weekdays and do Saturdays, so we can catch you both."

If not

"No worries at all — just wanted to check."

8 — Appointment Close

Offer the question, then wait. The first person to speak after this question loses.

"What time are you & your partner home? When do they get home?"

Offer the soonest slot first, ideally tomorrow, and give two options: "I've got 12 or 2 — which is better?" Both sales this week were next-day bookings. Not sure of a time? "Better to have something down than nothing — you can always reschedule."

"Perfect. And just grab your address for me?"

Spell the street back and confirm the suburb.

"Brilliant. So that's [Day] at [Time] — I'll send a confirmation through via text and email, with the time, address, a link to our site if you want to have a look beforehand, and a bit about who we are and what to expect when the technician arrives."

9 — Promo

Post-close only. The promotion is a reward for deciding — not a lever to extract the decision. Pre-close use cheapens the brand.

"Before I leave you to it — one thing worth mentioning — we do have a promotion running through [month]. If you decide on the day that you'd like to go ahead, there's up to 20% we can take off. Entirely up to you — no pressure at all."

If they say "I never decide on the day"

"Totally fair — the quote's in writing so you can take your time. The technician will explain how the promotion works on the day."

10 — Close Out

The final 30 seconds create the emotional memory of the call. Warm, organised, confident. This is your most powerful cancellation prevention tool.

"Anyways [Name], appreciate your time. Have a great rest of your day."

If they ask the price

It came up in most calls this week. "I don't have pricing in the office" lost a call, so answer it with confidence.

"Great question. Honestly, it depends on your home — the size of the system, where it goes, and your water pressure. That's why the technician prices it on the spot. You get a fixed quote in writing, no surprises, and we've got 0% interest plans so it's not one lump sum."

If they push again

"I can't give you a number that's right for your house without someone seeing it. But the test is free, the quote's free, and you keep the quote either way."

Hours, Speed & No-Pickups

Follow-up

Your hours & the 60-second rule

Standby: 9am to 6pm Monday to Friday, plus Saturday mornings till lunch. Saturday afternoons aren't worth calling — nobody picks up.

Leads land scattered through the day, and here's the thing that makes this whole role work: the moment someone enquires, they are literally on their phone. It's the one moment of the entire day you're guaranteed to reach them. Sixty seconds later they've put it down, opened something else, and your odds drop with every minute.

So between 9 and 6, the rule is firm: call within 60 seconds of the notification — or as close to it as humanly possible. If you're in the middle of something, it can wait sixty seconds; the lead can't. This isn't a nice-to-have, it's the job. The faster you call, the more you book, the more you earn. Simple.

After 6pm, Monday to Friday — your choice, and it's smart money. A lead that enquires at 7:30pm is just as warm as a 2pm one, and nobody else is calling them. You're never required to pick up the phone after 6 — but every evening lead you choose to call is a conversion the morning would have lost.

Any lead that lands outside your hours and doesn't get an evening call: 9am sharp, first thing, before anything else.

If they don't pick up — the 3-day chase

Day 1 — minimum 3 calls. Call within 60 seconds. No answer? Call again immediately — people miss the first ring constantly and answer the second. Still nothing: send Text 1 (below) and get on with your day. Then hit them again mid-afternoon, and once more towards the end of the day. Three separate moments minimum — someone busy at 11 is free at 5. No voicemail on day 1; keep them curious enough to answer.

Day 2 — two calls, voicemail and text. One morning, one afternoon. On the first, leave the voicemail (below) and send Text 2 right after it — the voicemail and the text land together, so your name's in two places on their phone.

Day 3 — last call, then park it. One final call at a time you haven't tried yet. No answer: send the final text and put the lead away. If they reply next week or next month, pick it up like no time has passed — they enquired once, they're still a homeowner with the same water.

That's eight touches in three days. It feels like a lot from your side; from theirs it reads as a company that actually wants their business. The leads you lose from chasing hard are leads that were never booking — the ones you win are pure profit.

Cancellations — always a phone call, never a text back

If a booked customer cancels or tries to, you call them the moment you see it. Never accept a cancellation by text — a text costs them nothing; a conversation makes them think again.

"Hey [Name], saw your message — wanted to catch you quickly because our technician's already going to be out your way that day. What's happened on your end?"

If it's a time problem: move the time, never the commitment —

"No stress at all — would earlier or later that day work better, or the day after? He's out that way anyway, so we've got a bit of room to move."

If it's cold feet: lower the stakes and hold the booking —

"Completely fair. Just remember there's nothing to decide on the day — it's a free test, you'll see exactly what's in your water, and worst case you've lost an hour and learnt something about your home. Let's keep your spot, and if the timing's genuinely wrong, we'll move it."

The goal of every cancellation call: walk away with a new time, or the old time kept. A reschedule is a save; a cancel is only ever the last resort.

Text 1 — straight after the first missed call

Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration — just tried to give you a quick call about your water enquiry. I'll try you again shortly, or reply here with a time that suits and I'll call you then. Thanks, Neptune Filtration!

Voicemail — day 2, first morning attempt

"Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration, following up on your water enquiry. I'll give you another try later today — or call us back on this number whenever suits. Talk soon."

Short, warm, no detail — the goal is a callback, not a pitch on tape.

Text 2 — day 2, with the voicemail

Hi [Name], [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration here — tried to reach you a couple of times about your water enquiry. Reply with a good time and I'll call you then. Thanks, Neptune Filtration!

Final text — day 3, after the last call

Hi [Name], we haven't been able to catch you about your water enquiry, so I'll leave it with you — reply here anytime and we'll pick it up from there. Thanks, Neptune Filtration!

When they finally pick up

"Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration, tried you a little earlier. How are you going?"

Then straight into the normal script from the top — same call, same flow. Never open with an apology or "sorry to chase you"; they enquired, you're simply getting back to them.
