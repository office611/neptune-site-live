The map: your job, start to finish

Read this first. Every lead goes through the same eight steps, and every step has a section further down this page. If you're ever unsure what to do next, come back here.

  1. A lead lands in GoHighLevelYou get a notification on the LeadConnector app. The lead shows up in Opportunities → In-Home Consultations → New Lead, usually titled "Name – Suburb". Video 2
  2. Call within 60 secondsThey're still holding their phone. Call straight from the lead's file so it shows our number. No answer: call again immediately, then follow the 3-day chase. Follow-up
  3. Run the scriptOpening → discovery → suburb → problem → prior testing → free test → book. Keep it about 5 minutes. The script
  4. Handle the pushbackPrice, "I've already had a test", "is this a sales pitch", "my partner can't make it". Every common one has an answer. Pushback
  5. Book it in the calendar while they're on the lineDay, time, full address (spell the street back), and who'll be there. The confirmation text and email go out automatically once it's booked. Videos 3 & 4
  6. Write the notesTheir concern (dry skin, taste, eczema), who's in the home, tap or filtered, any test already done, who the decision-makers are, and anything they asked that the technician needs to answer. Video 5
  7. Move the opportunity to the right stageBooked → Booked In Home. Spoke but couldn't book → Contacted – Could not Book In. No answer → Contacted – Did Not Answer. See the stage table below. Video 6
  8. Protect the bookingIf they cancel or try to, you call, never text back. Move the time, never the commitment. Cancellations · Video 7

The inbound script

This is the call, start to finish. Blue blocks are what you say; italics are how to play it. Green Updated tags mark changes made after reviewing real calls from 11–14 November. Under each step, ▶ plays a real clip of that moment.

1 — Opening

The first 8 seconds determine whether you get a conversation or a wall. Sound like a person, not a process.

"Hey, is this [Name]?"

Wait for confirmation.

"Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration. How are you going?"

Genuine pause — actually listen to their answer.

"Good to hear. So I'm just following up on your enquiry — wanted to ask a couple of quick questions and arrange a quote for you."

If a call-screening robot answers Updated

"[Your Name] from Neptune Filtration, returning your water filter enquiry."

Say what the call is about. "Neptune Filtration" on its own sounds like a cold call, and "returning your enquiry" gets you put through.

If they ask the price straight away Updated

"Great question, and I'll come back to that — just two quick questions first so I point you at the right setup."

Then you must come back to it. If you dodge it twice, you lose them. Use the price answer in Pushback.

▶ Hear it: "That was quick." This is why the 60-second rule works.

2 — Discovery

Before positioning anything — you need them talking. The more a customer speaks in the first two minutes, the more they trust you by the end of the call.

"So first off — are you having any main concerns with the water at the moment, or just looking for cleaner water in general?"

Listen fully. Don't interrupt. Don't pre-load.

If specific problem (skin / taste / smell)

"Yeah, that makes a lot of sense — honestly, it's more common than people realise. Most of the time people have been dealing with it for a while without ever connecting it back to the water."

Brief pause. Let it land. Then ask one follow-up: "Has that got worse over time, or has it always been like that?" It gets them talking, and it gives the technician something to work with.

If general / "just curious"

"Yeah, that's how most people start actually — just a feeling that the water could be better, or they've been reading more about it and want to know where they stand."
Health rule Updated — when someone mentions eczema, a child's skin condition, kidney issues or any illness, never say filtered water "will help" or "can fix" it. We're not doctors, and it sets up a promise the technician can't keep. Say instead: "I'm sorry to hear that. We hear about skin a lot. The technician can show you exactly what's in your water, and you can make up your own mind from there." Write the condition in the notes so the technician knows.
▶ Hear it: a customer whose daughter has eczema. Listen for the line to avoid. This booking later cancelled.

3 — Location, Ownership & Local Authority

These qualifying questions must feel like one flowing beat, not a form. The local authority line lands immediately after you have the suburb — not before.

"Whereabouts in Melbourne are you based?"

After answer — pause naturally, then:

"Yeah, we're actually out [suburb] way quite a bit."

One beat. Casual. No expansion yet. The Suburbs section lists the neighbouring suburbs for each area. Use them in step 4.

"And you're the homeowner as well?"

If yes — continue. If renting — polite close out.

If they haven't moved in yet Updated

"Oh congrats! Have you got the keys yet? Best time to do it is before you're living there. We'll test the water at the new place."

This came up twice in four days. Book the visit at the new address.

"If you don't mind me asking — roughly how many people are living in the home with you?"
"And are you mainly on tap water, bottled, or some sort of filtered water already?"

4 — Problem Anchor

This is the emotional centre of the call. Local credibility from Section 3 is what makes this land. Deliver at a slightly slower pace. Let the information breathe.

"One thing that comes up pretty consistently out [suburb] and surrounding [suburbs] is that the chlorine concentration is actually quite a bit higher than most people realise."

Half-beat pause.

"And it doesn't show up the same way for everyone. Some people notice it in the taste. Others feel it on their skin — dry patches, itchiness, sometimes it shows up in the hair and scalp. A lot of people have just gotten used to it over time without ever making the connection."
"Guess — anyone in the home experiencing something like this?"

If yes

"Yeah, that makes sense — once you understand what's actually going on with the water, it tends to click pretty quickly."

Don't over-expand. Let them sit with the connection.

If no

"Yeah, fair enough — it can be pretty subtle. Or it just becomes part of the background — people get used to it over time and stop noticing. Doesn't necessarily mean it's not there."

Do NOT mention "assessment" yet. Move to the next section.

▶ Hear it: naming the suburb and its neighbours (Avondale Heights / Essendon). The customer makes the connection himself: "we're both experiencing hair loss."

5 — Prior Testing

The knowledge gap question. It almost always reveals they've never had a proper assessment — and that admission is what makes the assessment feel necessary.

"Has anyone come by to conduct a water quality assessment before?"

If no — most common

"Yeah, that's pretty typical — most people haven't. So you've got a concern, you're managing it one way or another, but you don't actually have a clear picture of what's going on."

Pause. Let that sit. Then:

"That's exactly what we'd look to fix."

If yes (another company has already been out) Updated

This happened in 3 of 12 calls. They've usually got a quote already, so treat them as a comparison, not a first look.

"Oh nice — good that you've had that done. Did they leave you a quote?"
"The testing side of things is fairly similar across the board. Where it gets useful is actually having someone come out and look at your place — your plumbing setup, how many people are using the water, what you're trying to get out of it."
"A water test tells you what's in your water. What we're doing when we come out is working out what the right solution looks like for your home specifically — and you'll have a second fixed quote to compare side by side. Most people find that worth 30 minutes."
▶ Hear it: a customer who'd already had a test from another company. Still booked.

6 — Assessment Framing

By this point the customer has acknowledged a problem, revealed how they're imperfectly handling it, and admitted they've never properly tested their water. The assessment is now the obvious answer. Do not over-explain the product — that is the technician's job.

"What we typically recommend is to get your water tested — we offer this completely free. One of our technicians comes past your place, tests it, shows you the results on the spot, and gives you a suggestion if there's anything to address."
"From there, if there's anything that needs addressing, they'll recommend what suits your place and give you a fixed quote on the spot. It's in writing, so you can keep it and compare — there's no pressure to make any decision on the day." Updated

The bold part answers two worries before they come up: "is this a sales pitch?" and "I never decide on the day". Both came up last week.

"At the very least, you'll actually know what's in your water — which most people genuinely don't."

7 — Transition To Booking

Don't ask "would you like to book?" — that invites a "no." Treat the appointment as the self-evident next step.

"Let me see when someone can come by."

Brief pause — sounds like you're actually checking.

"While my calendar loads — just so you know, there's two small requirements: about 30 minutes of your time, which allows us to run the test and go through the product, and anyone else involved in the decision needs to be there too — so yourself and your partner, if that's relevant." Updated

Say "about 30 minutes", not "30 to 40". A range makes it sound longer and less organised.

If a partner exists

"Worth having them there if you can — just means you don't have to relay everything afterwards. The technician goes through it all in detail and gives the quote on the spot, so it's a lot smoother when everyone's across it together."

If the partner works late Updated

"No stress — we go till about 6:30 on weekdays and we do Saturdays too, so we can catch you both after work."

Offer the late slot and Saturday before they say "I'll just pass it on to them". That line saved two bookings last week.

If not

"No worries at all — just wanted to check."
▶ Hear it: the decision-maker is the customer's sister. Offering 6:30pm and Saturday keeps her in the booking.

8 — Appointment Close

Offer the question, then wait. The first person to speak after this question loses.

"What time are you & your partner home? When do they get home?"

Offer the soonest slot first, ideally tomorrow. If a technician is already in their area, say so: "We've got a technician out in [suburb] tomorrow, I can squeeze you in at 12." Both sales this week were next-day bookings. Updated

If they're not sure of a time Updated

"Better to have something down than nothing — you can always reschedule."

Once you know roughly when they're free, give them two options, not an open question: "I've got 12 or 2 — which is better?" Updated

"Perfect. And just grab your address for me?"

Spell the street back letter by letter and confirm the suburb. Half the addresses last week had unusual street names. A wrong address is a guaranteed no-show. Updated

"Brilliant. So that's [Day] at [Time] — I'll send a confirmation through via text and email, with the time, address, a link to our site if you want to have a look beforehand, and a bit about who we are and what to expect when the technician arrives."

If they ask for a calendar invite, say yes, then add their email to the contact before you book. The booking email has the time and address in it.

9 — Promo

Post-close only. The promotion is a reward for deciding — not a lever to extract the decision. Pre-close use cheapens the brand.

"Before I leave you to it — one thing worth mentioning — we do have a promotion running through [month]. If you decide on the day that you'd like to go ahead, there's up to 20% we can take off. Entirely up to you — no pressure at all."

If they say "I never decide on the day" or "do I lose the 20%?" Updated

"Totally fair — plenty of people like to sit on it. The quote's in writing, so you can take your time. The technician will explain exactly how the promotion works for you on the day."

Don't argue, and don't promise them the discount will still be there later. Move straight on to the close-out. It came up twice last week, and "that sucks" is not the answer.

▶ Hear it: "If we don't decide on the day, do we lose the 20%?" Listen for how it should have been answered.

10 — Close Out

The final 30 seconds create the emotional memory of the call. Warm, organised, confident. This is your most powerful cancellation prevention tool.

"Anyways [Name], appreciate your time. If anything changes, just call this number and we'll move it for you. Have a great rest of your day." Updated

"Call us and we'll move it" means they reschedule instead of cancelling.

If They Push Back — Or Say Yes

Pushback & what to say

These are the questions customers actually asked in the last 12 calls, most common first. Each one shows what to say, plus what to do whether they agree or keep pushing. Tap one to open it.

"How much does it cost?" — asked in 7 of 12 calls

This is the most important answer on the page. "I don't have pricing in the office" sounds like hiding something, and it lost a call outright last week. Answer it every time, with confidence.

First time they ask

"Great question. Honestly, it depends on your home — the size of the system, where it goes, and your water pressure. That's why the technician prices it on the spot. You get a fixed quote in writing, so there's no surprises, and we've got 0% interest plans so it doesn't have to be one lump sum."

If they push again ("just give me a rough idea")

"I get it — you don't want to waste 30 minutes if it's way off. I can't give you a number that's actually right for your house without someone seeing it. But the test is free, the quote's free, and you keep the quote either way. Worst case, you know exactly what's in your water and what it'd cost to fix."

If they agree

"Easy. Let me see when someone can come by…" (straight to step 7)

If they still say "just text me the price"

"No worries at all. I'll pop a note on your file, and if you want the exact number for your place, just reply to the text and I'll get a technician out."

Move them to Contacted – Could not Book In with the note "wanted price". Call back in 2–3 days.

▶ Hear it (lost): "Just tell me the price, that's my main concern." He hung up after the dodge.
▶ Hear it (saved): price asked, 0% plans mentioned, still booked. It's better with the new wording.
"How are you different? Five companies called me."

Last week's answer ("we're a big Australian company", "one of the biggest in Melbourne") can't be backed up, and customers can tell. Use these facts instead. They're all true.

"Fair question — there's a few of us. Three things: we're Melbourne-based and our systems are installed by licensed Melbourne plumbers; the system is WaterMark certified to the Australian standard; and the install comes with a 7-year workmanship warranty. The technician will walk you through the rest — and you'll have a written quote to compare against the others."

If they agree

Book it. Put "comparing with other companies" in the notes so the technician comes ready.

If they push ("but what's actually different about the filter?")

"That's exactly what the technician's best at — he'll show you side by side on the day. I'd rather you hear it from him than get half the story from me."
▶ Hear it: the question as customers really ask it. Compare the answer given with the one above.

"Oh nice — that's great, it means you're already across it. Did they leave you a quote? The way most people use us is as the second opinion — our technician looks at your plumbing and pressure, and you get a second fixed quote to compare side by side."

"Fair question. The technician tests your water, shows you the results, and leaves you a fixed quote in writing. If it's not for you, no dramas — you've still found out what's in your water for free."

If they agree

Note "wants low-pressure visit, quote to review" for the technician.

If they push

"Totally get it. Nobody's going to make you decide anything on the day — that's a promise."

"My partner / sister can't be there"
"No stress — we go till about 6:30 on weekdays and do Saturdays, so we can work around them. When are you both home?"

If they say "I'll just pass it on to them"

"You could, but honestly the technician goes through a lot — results, options, the quote — and it's much easier for everyone to hear it first-hand. It saves you a second conversation."

If they insist they decide alone

Accept it, and write "sole decision-maker (confirmed)" in the notes.

"What do you do with my details?"
"Good question. It's only used for your appointment messages — we don't sell or share it, and we won't spam you."
▶ Hear it: answered well. Calm, short, and the customer relaxes.
"Do you make the filters, or are you just a sales company?"
"We're a Melbourne filtration company — we test, supply and install, and the install is done by licensed plumbers with a 7-year workmanship warranty. The technician can show you the system itself on the day."

Never claim we're "the biggest" or make up numbers. Stick to what's true.

When they say yes — lock it in

A booking you don't firm up can still slip away. When they agree:

  • Book it in GoHighLevel while they're still on the line.
  • Read back the day, time and full address.
  • Confirm who'll be there.
  • Tell them the confirmation text is on its way and to call this number if anything changes.
  • Stop talking. Do the promo line, then the close-out, and hang up. Extra chat after a yes just gives them room for doubts.

Keep doing

  • Next-day bookings. Both sales this week (Doreen, Clyde) were booked for the next day. Always offer the soonest slot first.
  • Speed. Customers notice ("That was quick!") and stay friendly.
  • Naming the suburb and its neighbours before the chlorine line. It makes us sound local, and people connect their own symptoms to it.
  • Getting the partner there nearly every time, with late slots and Saturdays offered.
  • "While my calendar loads…" It moves into booking without asking permission.
  • Specific time offers ("12 or 2?") instead of "when suits you?"
  • The data-privacy answer. Short, calm and honest.

Fix now

  • Price dodging. "I don't have pricing in the office" was said in 5 calls and lost one outright. Use the price answer.
  • Health promises. "Filtered water can definitely help" with a child's eczema. That booking later cancelled. Follow the health rule.
  • Making things up. "One of the biggest in Melbourne" and "a big Australian company". Use the true facts instead.
  • "On the day" pressure. Two people pushed back on the promo. Lead with "the quote's in writing, you can keep it".
  • "30 to 40 minutes" becomes "about 30 minutes".
  • Addresses. Spell the street back every time.
  • Callbacks. "Call me in an hour" got a call 7 hours later. Set a task and call on time.
  • Matching the customer. A direct customer said "you make it sound like rocket science". Shorten the call for people like that.

Hours, Speed & No-Pickups

Follow-up

Your hours & the 60-second rule

Standby: 9am to 6pm Monday to Friday, plus Saturday mornings till lunch. Saturday afternoons aren't worth calling — nobody picks up.

Leads land scattered through the day, and here's the thing that makes this whole role work: the moment someone enquires, they are literally on their phone. It's the one moment of the entire day you're guaranteed to reach them. Sixty seconds later they've put it down, opened something else, and your odds drop with every minute.

So between 9 and 6, the rule is firm: call within 60 seconds of the notification — or as close to it as humanly possible. If you're in the middle of something, it can wait sixty seconds; the lead can't. This isn't a nice-to-have, it's the job. The faster you call, the more you book, the more you earn. Simple.

After 6pm, Monday to Friday — your choice, and it's smart money. A lead that enquires at 7:30pm is just as warm as a 2pm one, and nobody else is calling them. You're never required to pick up the phone after 6 — but every evening lead you choose to call is a conversion the morning would have lost.

Any lead that lands outside your hours and doesn't get an evening call: 9am sharp, first thing, before anything else.

If they don't pick up — the 3-day chase

Day 1 — minimum 3 calls. Call within 60 seconds. No answer? Call again immediately — people miss the first ring constantly and answer the second. Still nothing: send Text 1 (below) and get on with your day. Then hit them again mid-afternoon, and once more towards the end of the day. Three separate moments minimum — someone busy at 11 is free at 5. No voicemail on day 1; keep them curious enough to answer.

Day 2 — two calls, voicemail and text. One morning, one afternoon. On the first, leave the voicemail (below) and send Text 2 right after it — the voicemail and the text land together, so your name's in two places on their phone.

Day 3 — last call, then park it. One final call at a time you haven't tried yet. No answer: send the final text and put the lead away. If they reply next week or next month, pick it up like no time has passed — they enquired once, they're still a homeowner with the same water.

That's eight touches in three days. It feels like a lot from your side; from theirs it reads as a company that actually wants their business. The leads you lose from chasing hard are leads that were never booking — the ones you win are pure profit.

Cancellations — always a phone call, never a text back

If a booked customer cancels or tries to, you call them the moment you see it. Never accept a cancellation by text — a text costs them nothing; a conversation makes them think again.

"Hey [Name], saw your message — wanted to catch you quickly because our technician's already going to be out your way that day. What's happened on your end?"

If it's a time problem: move the time, never the commitment —

"No stress at all — would earlier or later that day work better, or the day after? He's out that way anyway, so we've got a bit of room to move."

If it's cold feet: lower the stakes and hold the booking —

"Completely fair. Just remember there's nothing to decide on the day — it's a free test, you'll see exactly what's in your water, and worst case you've lost half an hour and learnt something about your home. Let's keep your spot, and if the timing's genuinely wrong, we'll move it."

The goal of every cancellation call: walk away with a new time, or the old time kept. A reschedule is a save; a cancel is only ever the last resort.

Text 1 — straight after the first missed call

Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration — just tried to give you a quick call about your water enquiry. I'll try you again shortly, or reply here with a time that suits and I'll call you then. Thanks, Neptune Filtration!

Voicemail — day 2, first morning attempt

"Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration, following up on your water enquiry. I'll give you another try later today — or call us back on this number whenever suits. Talk soon."

Short, warm, no detail — the goal is a callback, not a pitch on tape.

Text 2 — day 2, with the voicemail

Hi [Name], [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration here — tried to reach you a couple of times about your water enquiry. Reply with a good time and I'll call you then. Thanks, Neptune Filtration!

Final text — day 3, after the last call

Hi [Name], we haven't been able to catch you about your water enquiry, so I'll leave it with you — reply here anytime and we'll pick it up from there. Thanks, Neptune Filtration!

When they finally pick up

"Hey [Name] — it's [Your Name] from Neptune Filtration, tried you a little earlier. How are you going?"

Then straight into the normal script from the top — same call, same flow. Never open with an apology or "sorry to chase you"; they enquired, you're simply getting back to them.
